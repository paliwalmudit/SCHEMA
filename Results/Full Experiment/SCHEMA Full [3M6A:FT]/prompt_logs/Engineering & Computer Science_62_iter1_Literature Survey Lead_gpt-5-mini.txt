- Refined taxonomy with formal definitions
  - Drift types: 
    - Covariate/input drift: p(x) changes while p(y|x) remains constant.
    - Label/prior drift: p(y) changes while p(x|y) remains constant.
    - Conditional/concept drift: p(y|x) changes, possibly with p(x) and p(y) individually changing.
    - Appearance vs. semantic drift: appearance drift alters style/stroke/lighting without changing semantics; semantic drift changes the meaning or mapping of classes (label semantics shift).
  - Supervision, input level, detection mechanism, adaptation strategy, deployment constraints, and evaluation protocol as orthogonal axes, enabling a matrix that maps each paper to its regime.

- Theoretical guarantees and limits
  - Establish identifiability conditions: when can a detector distinguish covariate vs conditional drift given finite labeled data and latent representations?
  - Impossibility/limitation results: under high-dimensional image spaces and small drift magnitudes, detection guarantees deteriorate; quantify sample complexity and false alarm bounds.
  - Stability and recoverability: characterize conditions under which fast adaptation preserves performance after drift onset, with bounds on regret in online settings.

- Formal tests and experimental scenarios to distinguish drift types
  - Latent-space tests: monitor distributions of deep latent embeddings and of class-conditional embeddings; use nonparametric two-sample tests (MMD, energy distance) and classifier-based two-sample tests on z = fθ(x).
  - Conditional drift tests: compare p(y|x) estimates before/after drift using probabilistic calibration or density-ratio teachers; employ conditional distribution tests such as KS/MMD on p(y|x) strata or likelihood ratio tests with plug-in models.
  - Sequential change-point detectors: adapt BOCPD, CUSUM, Page-Hinkley to streaming image features with latency controls.
  - Localization: develop tests that flag affected classes, regions, or attributes, enabling explainable drift attribution (per-class drift metrics, region-based ablation).

  - Metrics: detection delay, true/false alarm rates, precision-recall of alarms, post-drift accuracy, adaptation latency, compute/memory footprint, and annotation cost.
