Simulator-fidelity checklist (minimum recommended settings):
- Physics timestep: 0.001s (1 ms) or smaller; ensure control loop matches real robot (commonly 500–1000 Hz). Use multiple solver substeps for contact resolution.

- Actuator model: include motor dynamics (first-order filters), torque limits, torque-rate limits, gearbox friction, and series elasticity if present on hardware.
- Sensors and latency: add measurement noise (Gaussian + bias), quantization, and control/communication latency (5–20 ms typical).
- Joint friction and backlash: model coulomb/viscous friction and range limits.
- Solver tolerances: use strict tolerances to avoid unrealistic energy gains/losses.

- Two-stage approach: run system identification first (calibrated mass, inertia, friction, actuator response) using real trajectories; then perform domain randomization around identified parameters.

- Progressive/randomization schedule: start narrow during training, progressively widen (“curriculum randomization”), and include worst-case samples late in training to encourage robustness.

Common success patterns and failure modes:
- Success: careful ID + modest randomization + actuator and contact realism lead to reliable zero-shot or few-shot transfer (examples: Cassie, Digit demos following ID-informed sim).

- Tasks: flat-speed tracking, rough/uneven terrain, stairs, gap crossing, and push-recovery.
