  "Related Work": "Existing approaches include (1) design-effect formulas for clustered data that scale variance by 1+(m-1)·ICC (ICC = intraclass correlation, the share of total variance due to clusters), (2) Satterthwaite and Kenward–Roger small-sample df and variance adjustments for linear mixed models, (3) delta-method linearization and first-order Laplace approximations for generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs), (4) simulation-based tools (for example, packages that simulate data under specified mixed models), and (5) measurement-error corrections that disattenuate effects using reliability estimates.   "Abstract": "We address the problem that common analytic power formulas for clustered linear and generalized linear mixed models can be inaccurate in practical behavioral-science designs, especially when cluster counts are small, cluster sizes are unbalanced, outcomes are nonnormal or rare, or measures have limited reliability. Our approach combines closed-form variance and noncentrality approximations with Kenward–Roger small-sample adjustments, explicit measurement-reliability (disattenuation) corrections, and prespecified validity envelopes that flag regions requiring simulation. We benchmark analytic outputs against large Monte Carlo grids and against established simulation tools, using deterministic seeds and MCSE targets to ensure reproducibility. Main contributions: (1) a catalog of analytic formulas with domains of validity, (2) quantified sample-size inflation rules for ICC misspecification and measurement noise, and (3) a reproducible validation protocol with unit tests and runtime budgets. Expected outcome: practical, testable guidelines and software-ready formulas that reduce power-estimation error in routine behavioral designs and clearly indicate when simulation or Bayesian latent integration is necessary.",

  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Technical failures: nonconvergence of mixed-model optimizers, singular or boundary variance estimates, and unstable Laplace approximations for strong random effects.
