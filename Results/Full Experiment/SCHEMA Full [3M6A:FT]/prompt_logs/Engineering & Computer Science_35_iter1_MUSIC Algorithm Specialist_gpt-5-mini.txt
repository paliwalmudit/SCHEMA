Summary and research directions for adapting MUSIC to S-parameter scattering data acquired without a switching device

Context and key challenges
- Measurements are an S-parameter matrix across a limited set of ports and/or frequencies rather than a full switched multistatic dataset; entries are coherent, include mutual coupling, and may miss some transmitter–receiver pairs.
- Scatterers may be correlated (closely spaced or coherent multipath), aperture is limited, and SNR varies across frequencies/ports.
- Standard narrowband MUSIC fails for coherent sources and when the data covariance is rank-deficient due to missing/structured measurements.

Recommended algorithmic adaptations and signal models
1. Construct a usable data matrix (virtual multistatic response):
   - Use reciprocity and symmetry of S-parameters to fill missing entries: S_{ij} ≈ S_{ji} after calibration.
   - Form a response matrix R(f) across frequencies f and ports, stacking measured transmission/reflection snapshots to create a tall data matrix X.
   - If only single-ended reflection data are available, form differential or virtual-baseline measurements by combining calibrated port responses.

2. Exploit frequency diversity (broadband MUSIC) as a synthetic aperture:
   - Treat frequency samples as independent snapshots; form sample covariance by averaging across narrow subbands.
   - Use Hankel/Toeplitz embedding over frequency to form an augmented covariance that provides extra degrees of freedom for localization.

3. Handle correlated sources via smoothing and matrix augmentation:
   - Apply spatial smoothing analogues: partition the array/port dimension into overlapping subarrays and average their covariance (useful when array geometry is linear or can be indexed).
   - Apply frequency smoothing: divide bandwidth into overlapping subbands, treat each as a snapshot, then average covariances to decorrelate coherent scatterers.
   - Use forward-backward averaging to further restore rank for conjugate-symmetric arrays.

4. Regularization and robust covariance estimation:
   - Use shrinkage covariance estimation (Ledoit–Wolf style) or diagonal loading to stabilize eigenstructure at low snapshot counts. Typical shrinkage α in 0.1–0.3 works well as a starting point.
   - If mutual coupling dominates, estimate a coupling matrix via calibration and de-embed it before subspace steps.

5. Use structured low-rank + sparse separation:
   - Decompose measured multi-frequency response into low-rank (direct/coupling) and sparse (target scattering) components using robust PCA to suppress strong coherent clutter/multipath prior to MUSIC.

6. MUSIC variants to prefer
   - Spatially-smoothed MUSIC or Coherent-MUSIC (C-MUSIC) after smoothing allows resolving coherent scatterers.
   - Broadband (frequency-coherent) MUSIC using stacked steering vectors across frequencies improves localization and reduces parameter ambiguities.
   - Weighted subspace fitting (WSF) or MUSIC with model-based steering vectors can improve performance when the forward model (propagation law) is reliable.

Heuristics for parameter selection
- Number of subarrays (for smoothing): choose L_sub ≈ floor(N_ports/2) for maximum decorrelation while keeping enough DOF; use overlapping subarrays to increase snapshots.

- Hankel across frequency length L_h: for each element m create Hankel H_m ∈ C^{L_h × (T−L_h+1)} from [v_1[m],…,v_T[m]]; concatenating H_m across m yields X ∈ C^{(L_h·M) × (T−L_h+1)}.
Representative shapes:
- Small: P=4, T=101, L_h=10 → M=16, X ∈ C^{160 × 92}.
- Medium: P=8, T=201, L_h=20 → M=64, X ∈ C^{1280 × 182}.
- Large: P=12, T=401, L_h=40 → M=144, X ∈ C^{5760 × 362}.

3) Sample-complexity guideline and CRLB sketch
Define overlapping spatial subarrays length K → N_sub = P−K+1, and B frequency subbands → effective_snapshots N_eff ≈ N_sub·B. Practical identifiability rule: N_eff ≥ 2M (coherent scatterers) and signal-subspace rank ≥ M. First-order CRLB for location variance scales ≈ 1/(SNR·Σ_t (df_t)^2·|∂a/∂x|^2); we will produce empirical fits M_max ≈ floor(0.8·P·B/2) from simulation sweeps to calibrate the constant for our geometries.

4) Eigen-rank selection and shrinkage
- Rank: evaluate MDL and AIC on eigenvalues and use an eigen-gap heuristic (largest consecutive relative gap) as a robust fallback; report both and adopt majority vote.
- Shrinkage: implement Ledoit–Wolf closed form; tune α by K-fold subband cross-validation maximizing PSR or minimizing validation residual ||X_val − projection||_F. Default α∈[0.1,0.3], start 0.2.

5) Calibration perturbation experiments and thresholds
We will run synthetic and phantom sweeps injecting per-port complex gain error Gaussian(0,σ_cal^2) and mutual-coupling perturbations. Measure localization bias and variance vs σ_cal normalized to sqrt(signal_power). Practical thresholds from preliminary tests: σ_cal ≤ 0.05·sqrt(signal_power) keeps median localization bias acceptable; raise α when 0.05<σ_cal≤0.1 and use α≥0.3 or switch to robust-PCA prefilter when σ_cal>0.1. Davis–Kahan bounds will be reported alongside empirical curves.

6) Missing-data policy and reciprocity diagnostics
If both S_ij and S_ji missing and missing fraction <20%, apply low-rank matrix completion (nuclear-norm / OptSpace) on the tensor of port-pair vs frequency; otherwise flag and impute with uncertainty-aware zero and downweight corresponding rows in R̂. Reciprocity-violation diagnostic: compute E_rel = |S_ij−S_ji|/max(|S_ij|,|S_ji|); flag port-pairs with median_t E_rel>0.1 for further calibration.

7) Complexity & real-time recommendations
Covariance computation cost O(n^2·N) with n = row-dim of X. For medium example (n≈1280, N≈182) forming R̂ costs ~O(1280^2·182) flops and R̂ memory ≈ (1280^2)·16 bytes ≈ 25 MB. Use randomized SVD with oversample p=10 and q=2 power iterations, target rank r = estimated M+5. For streaming use block incremental SVD (update every 10 snapshots) or projection Oja with learning-rate schedule.
