where R(Z) approximates the expected bitrate as estimated by the entropy model, D_i measures task-specific distortion (e.g., classification cross-entropy, detection bounding box regression loss, segmentation IoU gaps), and D_{pix} captures pixel-level reconstruction distortion (L1 plus perceptual loss). The weights \alpha_i and \beta control the trade-off emphasis between tasks and pixel fidelity under a target bitrate.

Crucially, to better represent the information bottleneck principle, we can interpret the bottleneck Z as a representation capturing only task-relevant information under a rate constraint. We may thus alternatively cast optimization as:

min_Z I(X;Z) - \sum_i \lambda_i I(Z; Y_i)

where I(·;·) denotes mutual information, encouraging Z to compress X but retain maximal predictive information about tasks Y_i. In practice, variational approximations to these terms can be incorporated via rate and task losses.

L = \sum_{l=1}^L [ R(Z_l|Z_{<l}) + \sum_i \alpha_i D_i(\hat{Y}_{i,l}, Y_i^{gt}) + \beta D_{pix, l}(X, \hat{X}_l) ]

Rate-Distortion & Information Theorist: "Building on the comprehensive transvfc framework proposal and codec engineering considerations, I propose a principled rate-distortion-task (RDT) optimization formulation that explicitly links the bitrate, feature reconstruction distortion, and downstream task performance in a unified loss. This formulation will guide training and offer theoretical interpretability of trade-offs, complementing the system design and empirical validation.

Formally, let X denote the input video, Z the compressed bottleneck representation, and T_i represent a downstream task i with output prediction \hat{Y}_i. Our objective is to minimize the weighted sum:

L = R(Z) + \sum_i \alpha_i D_i(\hat{Y}_i, Y_i^{gt}) + \beta D_{pix}(X, \hat{X})

where R(Z) approximates the expected bitrate as estimated by the entropy model, D_i measures task-specific distortion (e.g., classification cross-entropy, detection bounding box regression loss, segmentation IoU gaps), and D_{pix} captures pixel-level reconstruction distortion (L1 plus perceptual loss). The weights \alpha_i and \beta control the trade-off emphasis between tasks and pixel fidelity under a target bitrate.

Crucially, to better represent the information bottleneck principle, we can interpret the bottleneck Z as a representation capturing only task-relevant information under a rate constraint. We may thus alternatively cast optimization as:

min_Z I(X;Z) - \sum_i \lambda_i I(Z; Y_i)

where I(·;·) denotes mutual information, encouraging Z to compress X but retain maximal predictive information about tasks Y_i. In practice, variational approximations to these terms can be incorporated via rate and task losses.

For scalable coding with hierarchical tokens, we should explicitly model incremental rate-distortion benefits by decomposing Z into layers Z = {Z_1, Z_2, ..., Z_L} with increasing fidelity. This enables progressive decoding and graceful degradation. The loss can then be further refined to account for layer-wise contributions:

L = \sum_{l=1}^L [ R(Z_l|Z_{<l}) + \sum_i \alpha_i D_i(\hat{Y}_{i,l}, Y_i^{gt}) + \beta D_{pix, l}(X, \hat{X}_l) ]

where \hat{Y}_{i,l} and \hat{X}_l denote task and pixel predictions reconstructed from the first l layers.

To ensure robustness across bitrates and tasks, I recommend a multi-rate, multi-task curriculum that adaptively samples quantizer sizes and task subsets during training, combined with contrastive and teacher-student distillation losses. These can be mathematically formulated as auxiliary constraints that preserve task-relevant invariances and reduce divergence from full-resolution task models, accelerating convergence and improving generalization.
