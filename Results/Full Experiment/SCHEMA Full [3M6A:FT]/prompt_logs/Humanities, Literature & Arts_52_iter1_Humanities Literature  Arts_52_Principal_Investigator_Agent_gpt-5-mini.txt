  "Experiments": "Datasets: 1) plant-level cement/clinker production and fuel use from CEADs, provincial filings, and yearbooks; 2) customs/port throughput, China Railway freight stats, and enterprise shipment reports for material flows; 3) city-year construction outputs (completed floor area, construction value); 4) Sentinel-2/Landsat annual built-up footprints and cadastral samples; 5) provincial grid emission factors and NDRC/MEP inventory totals; 6) policy rollout timing and prefab factory records. Baselines: A) standard output-oriented DEA ignoring undesirable outputs; B) slack-based DEA without unexpected-output formulation; C) SFA (bad-output specification); D) Malmquist index computed with fixed national EFs (no calibration). Main method: unexpected-output SBM-DEA (CO2 as undesirable output) and Malmquist (Malmquist-Luenberger) index for dynamic change. Uncertainty: Monte Carlo 10,000 iterations with correlated draws (Gaussian copula/Cholesky). Ablation studies: 1) calibrated plant-level EFs vs fixed national EFs; 2) parcel-level BOM downscaling vs city-aggregate allocation; 3) include vs exclude transport embodied emissions. Causal tests: staggered DID/event-study for prefab or subsidy rollouts, synthetic control for single-city interventions, IV using distance to major cement/steel plants or port as instrument; report first-stage F-statistics and placebo tests. Evaluation metrics: DEA efficiency scores, percent avoidable CO2 = (1−efficiency)×observed CO2, Malmquist index and decomposition (efficiency change, technical change), bootstrap 95% CI for scores, RMSE and bias vs NDRC/CEADs totals, Sobol first- and total-order sensitivity indices, power and MDE tables for causal designs, and falsification/placebo p-values. Experimental setup: apply methods to all prefecture-level cities in Guangdong, Fujian, Zhejiang for 2013–2022; compute city-year scores, ML indices, and run Monte Carlo uncertainty; validate bottom-up totals against official inventories with holdout cities; report tabulated comparisons and graphs for ablations.",
  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "1) Data gaps: plant-level fuel use or provincial trade tables may be incomplete, causing biased EF calibration and flow matrices. 2) Temporal inconsistency: satellite-built maps may be available only to 2020, increasing uncertainty for 2021–2022 attribution. 3) Spatial misclassification: ML typology errors where cadastral data are absent can misallocate materials; confusion matrices may reveal low accuracy. 4) Double-counting or boundary errors: unclear lifecycle boundaries (embodied materials vs producing-industry emissions) can distort totals. 5) Causal identification limits: instruments (distance to plants/ports) may violate exclusion restrictions or be weak; small treated-sample sizes can reduce power. 6) Computational cost: Monte Carlo (10,000 draws) combined with Sobol analysis and bootstrap DEA is compute-intensive and may require substantial cloud resources. 7) Model sensitivity: DEA results depend on input/output choice and scaling; SFA assumptions (noise distribution) may not hold. 8) Generalization: findings for the Guangdong–Fujian–Zhejiang agglomeration may not transfer to other regions with different supply chains or construction practices. 9) Ethical and access constraints: NDAs and provincial data confidentiality may limit public release of raw datasets, restricting reproducibility to synthetic subsets."
