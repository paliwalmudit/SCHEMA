  "Abstract": "We address the challenge of trace carbon monoxide (CO) detection at low power by developing a fabrication route to stabilize nanostructured cubic-phase tin dioxide (SnO2) thin films using a simple sol–gel process combined with spin coating and rapid thermal anneal. The project integrates structural verification (grazing-incidence XRD, TEM), operando surface spectroscopy, and density functional theory (DFT) calculations to link surface energetics to sensor signals. Films are integrated into low-voltage metal-oxide-semiconductor (MOS) devices and evaluated under controlled humidity and interferent exposure. Main contributions are: (1) a reproducible thin-film route producing measurable cubic-phase fractions at ambient conditions, (2) experimental demonstration of selective CO sensing at trace levels with defined statistical confidence, and (3) DFT-derived descriptors (CO adsorption energy, vacancy formation) mapped to operando observables to guide functionalization. Expected outcome is a validated materials–device workflow that lowers CO limit-of-detection to environmental targets (≤100 ppb) while providing mechanistic descriptors for further optimization. (DFT = density functional theory; a quantum method to compute surface energies.)",

  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Phase-stability risk: cubic SnO2 may revert to tetragonal phase during anneal, aging, or device operation, yielding low cubic fraction and invalidating hypothesis. Scale and reproducibility: sol–gel + spin coating may produce batch-to-batch variability in porosity and thickness that raises variance and reduces statistical power. Sensitivity and selectivity limits: CO signals may be confounded by H2 or NO2 adsorption leading to false positives; noble-metal loadings may change conductivity and obscure ΔR/R0. Measurement-system errors: MFC nonlinearity, dead-volume, or calibration drift can bias concentration values and LOD estimates. DFT transferability: computed adsorption energies may not fully capture real surface disorder, hydroxylation, or multi-molecule coverage, limiting predictive accuracy. Power and thermal constraints: achieving low-power operation at elevated sensing temperatures may be infeasible for some functionalized films. Long-term stability: baseline drift, contact degradation, or adhesion failure may occur beyond 30 days; accelerated-aging may not exactly mimic field failure modes. Statistical risk: if observed effect sizes (ΔR/R0 per ppm) are smaller or noise larger than assumed, planned n may be insufficient to reach 95% CI targets."
