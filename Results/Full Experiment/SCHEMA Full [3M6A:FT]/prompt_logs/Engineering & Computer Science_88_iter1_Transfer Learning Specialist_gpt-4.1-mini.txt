Furthermore, adopting a dual independent screening workflow with Cohen’s kappa targets ≥0.70 and explicit reconciliation procedures enhances reliability and mitigates subjectivity in inclusion decisions. This supports rigorous validation of transfer learning claims and contextualizes reported algorithmic improvements. The well-structured non-English literature workflow, leveraging machine translation with confidence score thresholds and escalation to native reviewers, addresses a key gap in past reviews and mitigates language bias—a crucial consideration given substantial NIA research from diverse linguistic communities.

The fuzzy deduplication strategy combining deterministic keys and clearly defined token_set_ratio thresholds, coupled with a principled preference for published peer-reviewed versions, preserves data integrity and enables accurate lineage tracking across preprint and formal publications. Integrating continuous integration (CI) tests that verify hit counts, seed recall stability, and deduplication performance at each commit and on a weekly cadence further enforces reproducibility and mitigates pipeline drift during iterative expansions.

On the transfer-learning theory and validation front, I recommend concurrently operationalizing a detailed taxonomy—categorizing transfer as feature, instance, parameter, representation/meta-knowledge, and multi-task transfer—mapped explicitly onto concrete instantiations within major NIA families. This taxonomy should be integrated into the data extraction templates to systematically classify each study’s transfer approach, facilitating meta-analytic subgrouping and illuminating underexplored niches. Additionally, the protocol should emphasize extracting and evaluating studies’ transfer assumptions, including task relatedness justification and negative transfer risk mitigation strategies, as these are pivotal for meaningful knowledge synthesis and guiding principled future algorithm design.

Lastly, the integration of task-similarity metrics based on both problem-landscape features and algorithmic performance fingerprints is urgently needed to elevate validation rigor beyond superficial claims. Encouraging or requiring evaluated papers to report such metrics will enable meta-level insights about when transfer learning truly benefits NIAs. Together with the planned multi-level meta-analysis using robust effect-size conversions, these practices can crystallize best practices and open challenges for the field.

In summary, the current protocol locks robust thresholds and workflows foundational to a thorough, auditable systematic review. My role going forward will focus on mapping identified transfer-learning methods onto NIA use cases within the extraction and synthesis stages, critiquing validation rigor relative to transfer assumptions, and formulating best-practice recommendations to foster principled integration of transfer learning in nature-inspired optimization. I look forward to proceeding with the seed-set pilot, confident that the well-documented framework and reproducibility infrastructure in OSF and Docker will enable collaborative, transparent progress in this important interdisciplinary research endeavor.
