Missing Data Theorist: "Formal problem statement. Consider a multimodal dataset with modalities X1,...,Xk, a target Y, and missingness indicators R1,...,Rk where Rj = 1 if modality j is observed and 0 if missing. Each modality has a missing-rate profile that may be highly imbalanced across samples and potentially depend on the target or other observed modalities, i.e., MNAR in a structured, per-modality way. The learning objective is to estimate the conditional distribution p(Y | X1,…,Xk, R1,…,Rk) or a predictive function f(X1,…,Xk) robust to the irregular missingness pattern. The key challenge is identifiability and reliable generalization when missingness is nonrandom and highly imbalanced across modalities.

Theoretical properties and identifiability. Under MCAR or MAR within each modality conditional on available data and the missingness metadata, the observed-data likelihood can be identified from the complete-data likelihood via standard missing-data theory, and consistent estimation is possible with appropriate models. Under MNAR, identifiability generally fails without additional assumptions or auxiliary information. A formal goal is to delineate identifiability classes for MCE: (i) MAR-within-modality given a missing-rate embedding, (ii) MNAR with missingness depending on observed but not on unobserved modality content, (iii) MNAR with dependence on unobserved content but addressable via auxiliary proxies or conditional independence restrictions. We ask for conditions under which the joint distribution of all modalities and Y is partially identifiable, and for what functional targets (predictive mean, distribution, or calibrated uncertainty) consistent estimation is possible.

Recovery guarantees and limits. One can derive error bounds that quantify how missing-rate imbalance degrades predictive accuracy and calibration. Using inverse-probability weighting by per-modality missingness, one can obtain bias-corrected estimators under MAR; under MNAR, the bounds will depend on the strength of the MNAR mechanism and the informativeness of auxiliary variables. A meaningful limit is that with severe MNAR and no instrument or side information linking missingness to unobserved content, universal recovery of the true joint distribution is impossible; however, partial recovery is feasible for predictive tasks with controllable sensitivity to missingness via uncertainty quantification.

Assumptions and implications for MCE. Theoretical work should formalize: (A) a per-modality missingness model Rj ⊥⊥ {X1,…,Xk,Y} conditional on a subset of observed modalities and the missing-rate embeddings; (B) bounded missingness imbalance across samples; (C) availability of auxiliary variables or perfect or near-perfect proxies for missing modalities. Under (A) and (B), probabilistic fusion with explicit conditioning on Rj and reliability embeddings yields consistent estimation of p(Y|observed data) and calibrated predictive uncertainty. Under (C), identifiability improves, enabling stronger recovery guarantees.

Research directions and hypotheses. Propose to: (i) characterize identifiability regions for MCE under varying MNAR structures and missing-rate imbalances; (ii) derive generalization and calibration bounds as functions of per-modality missing rates, entropy of missingness, and modality reliability; (iii) analyze the impact of the MAG and MoE routing on bias-variance trade-offs under MNAR; (iv) design synthetic benchmarks with known missingness mechanisms to validate identifiability and bounds; (v) test auxiliary information strategies (e.g., side channels, weak labels) to tighten recovery guarantees. Hypotheses: H1, MAR-within-modality and bounded missingness yield consistency and good calibration for MCE; H2, MNAR without auxiliary information yields identifiable only for predictive targets with quantified sensitivity; H3, incorporating reliable missingness embeddings and uncertainty modeling improves robustness and maintains calibrated coverage across extreme imbalance."

Missing Data Theorist: "Formal problem statement. Consider a multimodal dataset with modalities X1,...,Xk, a target Y, and missingness indicators R1,...,Rk where Rj = 1 if modality j is observed and 0 if missing. Each modality has a missing-rate profile that may be highly imbalanced across samples and potentially depend on the target or other observed modalities, i.e., MNAR in a structured, per-modality way. The learning objective is to estimate the conditional distribution p(Y | X1,…,Xk, R1,…,Rk) or a predictive function f(X1,…,Xk) robust to the irregular missingness pattern. The key challenge is identifiability and reliable generalization when missingness is nonrandom and highly imbalanced across modalities.

Theoretical properties and identifiability. Under MCAR or MAR within each modality conditional on available data and the missingness metadata, the observed-data likelihood can be identified from the complete-data likelihood via standard missing-data theory, and consistent estimation is possible with appropriate models. Under MNAR, identifiability generally fails without additional assumptions or auxiliary information. A formal goal is to delineate identifiability classes for MCE: (i) MAR-within-modality given a missing-rate embedding, (ii) MNAR with missingness depending on observed but not on unobserved modality content, (iii) MNAR with dependence on unobserved content but addressable via auxiliary proxies or conditional independence restrictions. We ask for conditions under which the joint distribution of all modalities and Y is partially identifiable, and for what functional targets (predictive mean, distribution, or calibrated uncertainty) consistent estimation is possible.

Recovery guarantees and limits. One can derive error bounds that quantify how missing-rate imbalance degrades predictive accuracy and calibration. Using inverse-probability weighting by per-modality missingness, one can obtain bias-corrected estimators under MAR; under MNAR, the bounds will depend on the strength of the MNAR mechanism and the informativeness of auxiliary variables. A meaningful limit is that with severe MNAR and no instrument or side information linking missingness to unobserved content, universal recovery of the true joint distribution is impossible; however, partial recovery is feasible for predictive tasks with controllable sensitivity to missingness via uncertainty quantification.

Assumptions and implications for MCE. Theoretical work should formalize: (A) a per-modality missingness model Rj ⊥⊥ {X1,…,Xk,Y} conditional on a subset of observed modalities and the missing-rate embeddings; (B) bounded missingness imbalance across samples; (C) availability of auxiliary variables or perfect or near-perfect proxies for missing modalities. Under (A) and (B), probabilistic fusion with explicit conditioning on Rj and reliability embeddings yields consistent estimation of p(Y|observed data) and calibrated predictive uncertainty. Under (C), identifiability improves, enabling stronger recovery guarantees.

Research directions and hypotheses. Propose to: (i) characterize identifiability regions for MCE under varying MNAR structures and missing-rate imbalances; (ii) derive generalization and calibration bounds as functions of per-modality missing rates, entropy of missingness, and modality reliability; (iii) analyze the impact of the MAG and MoE routing on bias-variance trade-offs under MNAR; (iv) design synthetic benchmarks with known missingness mechanisms to validate identifiability and bounds; (v) test auxiliary information strategies (e.g., side channels, weak labels) to tighten recovery guarantees. Hypotheses: H1, MAR-within-modality and bounded missingness yield consistency and good calibration for MCE; H2, MNAR without auxiliary information yields identifiable only for predictive targets with quantified sensitivity; H3, incorporating reliable missingness embeddings and uncertainty modeling improves robustness and maintains calibrated coverage across extreme imbalance."
