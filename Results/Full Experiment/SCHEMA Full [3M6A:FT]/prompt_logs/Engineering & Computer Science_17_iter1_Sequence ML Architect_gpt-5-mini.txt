- For local stationarity, choose a local analysis window of duration T such that the signal’s parameters are approximately constant on T. A practical requirement is that the instantaneous frequency change across the window satisfy |ḟ(t)| T ≤ Δf = 1/T, i.e. |ḟ(t)| ≤ 1/T^2. This provides an explicit time-window selection criterion given a bound on frequency modulation rate.  
- Bias arises from spectral leakage (window spectral shape and smoothing kernels); variance reduces with averaging or with orthogonal tapers (degrees-of-freedom increase). Consistency of estimators requires window/taper parameters that shrink bias faster than variance as sample size grows in locally stationary models.

Recommended transforms and parameter regimes by signal class
- Slowly varying narrowband modes (quasi-harmonic): STFT or multitaper spectrogram with long window T and K DPSS tapers. Choose K ≈ 2NW where W is desired half-bandwidth and N is window length in samples. This minimizes leakage and variance while preserving modal separation. The bias is controlled by the taper spectral concentration; variance ∝ 1/(2K).  
- Rapidly varying instantaneous frequency (chirps): continuous wavelet transform (CWT) with a mother wavelet whose time support matches desired Δt; use analytic wavelets (Morlet) to retain phase/instantaneous frequency. Use Cohen-class with adaptive smoothing kernel designed to suppress cross-terms while controlling bias; alternatively use multitapered Wigner-Ville or an adaptive time-varying filter bank.
