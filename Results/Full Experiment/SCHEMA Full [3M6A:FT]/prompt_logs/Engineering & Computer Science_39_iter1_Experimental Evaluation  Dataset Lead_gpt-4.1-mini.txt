For real datasets, I will coordinate collection in motion-capture facilities ensuring tightly synchronized event camera and IMU streams, with rigorous extrinsic and temporal calibration validated through established routines. These real-world datasets will span a range of motion dynamics, lighting, and scene textures to stress-test the event measurement model and jitter robustness under practical conditions.

Second, I will define a rich evaluation metric suite that extends beyond traditional pose RMSE to include event reprojection residual distributions, timing jitter impact quantification through controlled perturbations, and statistical consistency tests such as NEES. Computational profiling—capturing per-iteration latency, factor graph memory usage, and solver-switch condition diagnostics—will be standard to evaluate real-time feasibility on targeted hardware, including Jetson-class embedded systems. Evaluation protocols will enforce standardized initializations and fixed-lag smoothing windows to mirror real-world streaming scenarios and ensure fair, repeatable comparisons.

Third, I will design and execute systematic ablation experiments and robustness tests to isolate the contributions of key framework components. These include kernel order effects (second vs third-order SDE priors), knot-spacing granularity, hyperparameter learning strategies (fixed vs online adaptation), event factor density (dense vs adaptive micro-batching), and timing jitter handling methods (marginalization only vs optimized per-event δt). Robustness will be assessed by injecting synthetic IMU noise and bias drift as well as simulated event bursts and occlusions, evaluating algorithmic resilience and numerical stability. These studies will leverage fixed seeds and fully scripted pipelines to guarantee reproducibility.

Fourth, to foster transparent and reproducible science, I will prepare complete, version-controlled calibration protocols, dataset release formats, evaluation scripts, and baseline implementations. All experiments will run with fixed random seeds and produce logged known-good outputs. Integration tests will confirm consistency between calibration, theoretical Jacobians, and backend optimization.
