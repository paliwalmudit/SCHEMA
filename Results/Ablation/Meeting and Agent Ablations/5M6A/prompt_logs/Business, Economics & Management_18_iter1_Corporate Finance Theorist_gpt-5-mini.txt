Summary and baseline framework
I propose a parsimonious, transparent trade-off model in which a representative bank chooses an equity-capital ratio k (equity / assets) to maximize shareholder value net of the social/private costs and benefits of capital. Key ingredients: (i) asset risk (σ), (ii) private benefits of leverage (tax-like or return-on-equity amplification and agency rents), (iii) funding advantages of higher capital (lower funding spreads s(k)), (iv) bankruptcy/distress costs D(L) increasing in leverage L=1−k, (v) franchise value/implicit subsidies (G), and (vi) regulatory constraints (minimum k̄).

Baseline objective (static):
Maximize V(k) = (1 − p(k;σ))·Φ(k) + p(k;σ)·Ψ(k) − C_e(k)
where p(k;σ) is failure probability (∂p/∂k < 0, ∂p/∂σ > 0), Φ(k) is continuation value when solvent, Ψ(k) is recovery value in default, and C_e(k) captures direct costs of equity (tax-equivalent cost, agency dilution). Funding benefit enters via Φ(k) through s(k): higher k lowers s and thus increases net interest margin.

Key comparative statics (intuitive and sign-identified)
- Asset risk (σ) ↑ ⇒ optimal k* ↑. Higher volatility raises default probability and marginal benefit of equity.
- Distress cost D ↑ (or franchise value G ↑) ⇒ k* ↑. Bigger costs of failure make equity more valuable as insurance.
- Deposit insurance generosity or TBTF subsidy (implicit reduction in p or D) ↑ ⇒ k* ↓. Safety nets reduce private incentive to hold equity.
- Funding-market friction intensity (sensitivity ∂s/∂k larger) ↑ ⇒ k* ↑. If equity materially reduces funding spreads, its benefit rises.
- Tax-like benefits of debt or agency rent from leverage ↑ ⇒ k* ↓. Higher private return from leverage reduces desired equity.
- Regulatory minimum k̄ binds ⇒ k* = max(k_free, k̄). Effects of binding regulation interact with bank heterogeneity.

Testable implications and empirical strategies
1. Cross-section: Conditional on risk-weighted assets, banks with higher realized asset volatility or higher share of risky lending hold higher CET1 ratios. Instrument volatility with exogenous shocks to loan portfolios (commodity shocks, regional housing shocks).

3. Funding-channel: Banks with greater reliance on wholesale funding experience larger increases in capital after increases in market funding stress; exploit episodes of market-wide funding shock.
