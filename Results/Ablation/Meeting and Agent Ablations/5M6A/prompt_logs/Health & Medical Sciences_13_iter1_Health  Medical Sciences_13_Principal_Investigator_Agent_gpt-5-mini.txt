Our approach applies three-level multivariate meta-analysis when within-study covariances are available and study counts permit, and robust variance estimation with small-sample corrections when dependence or small k prevents full multivariate modeling. We pre-specify effect-size harmonization rules, bias and GRADE mapping, moderator sets (including interoperability, device maturity, and IT integration), and conversion formulas for ORs, HRs, and standardized β. The main contribution is a method-focused, reproducible blueprint that identifies influential predictors and evaluates heterogeneity, bias, and robustness across modeling choices. Expected outcomes include ranked predictors, assessment of dominant theoretical frameworks, and a reproducible analysis pipeline for future updates and policy translation.",

  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Small and heterogeneous study counts may prevent stable pooled estimates and limit power for meta-regressions. Dependent effects within studies (multiple outcomes) may be incompletely recoverable if within-study covariances are not reported. Converting ORs, HRs, and standardized βs to a common metric introduces approximation error and potential bias when original models are heavily adjusted. Publication bias and selective reporting may remain underpowered to detect small-study effects, especially when k is small. Missing variance and SD imputation can bias estimates if auxiliary information is weak. Coding of technical IoT features may suffer from poor reporting and low interrater reliability. Bayesian selection models and multivariate survival models require substantial compute and can fail to converge for small or sparse data; diagnostics may be inconclusive. Generalization is limited across care settings, device types, and low-resource contexts due to contextual heterogeneity."
