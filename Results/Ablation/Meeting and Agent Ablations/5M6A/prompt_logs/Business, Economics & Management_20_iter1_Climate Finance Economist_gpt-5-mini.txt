Climate risks influence bank equity values through two broad channels: (1) direct valuation of expected cash flows (credit losses, higher funding costs, lower net interest margin) and (2) changes in risk premia (higher equity beta, volatility, liquidity premia). ECB strategy and communications (monetary policy, collateral and asset purchase policy, supervisory guidance, targeted green operations) modulate these effects by altering macro-financial conditions, perceptions of sovereign/backstop support, and the private return-to-risk of green vs brown assets. Empirically useful decomposition separates physical risk (acute and chronic damages) from transition risk (policy, technology, demand shifts) and an ECB policy/communication channel that can either amplify or mitigate bank-level re-pricing.

Key hypotheses (testable)
H1 — Physical-risk hypothesis: Banks with greater exposure to geographically concentrated physical risk (loan collateral or borrower locations in high hazard zones) experience more negative stock returns following extreme-weather events, reflecting higher expected credit losses and provisioning needs.

H5 — Liquidity and market-risk amplification: Banks more reliant on wholesale funding or holding large mark-to-market trading books suffer larger contemporaneous equity declines when climate shocks trigger market dislocation and increased haircuts.
H6 — Contagion/systemic channel: Climate shocks to large banks or sovereigns propagate to other banks’ stock returns via interbank exposures, common asset holdings, and correlated credit risk, raising systemic risk premia.
