Theoretical Analysis & Convergence Specialist: "No feedback has been provided yet, so I will advance concrete theoretical directions and a rigorous problem framing to propel MoMA-AC. The goal is to formalize the learning objective, derive a policy-gradient theorem for preference-conditioned multi-agent control, and outline convergent MoMA-AC variants with a clear experimental plan.

Formal problem statement
- There are N agents. Each agent i selects action a_i from A_i conditioned on state s and its preference p_i in a compact set P_i, yielding policy π_i(a_i|s,p_i). The environment provides a vector reward r(s,a) ∈ R^M, where M is the number of objectives.
- The joint policy π = {π_i} induces a trajectory τ with cumulative scalarized return J(π) depending on a scalarization operator S: R^M → R. Preferences can be agent-specific or global; we allow p_i to influence S or to modulate the weighting in S.
- The objective is to maximize the expected scalarized return under decentralized execution but with centralized training of a vector-valued critic Q(s,a) ∈ R^M (one head per objective) and an aggregate policy gradient that respects the multi-objective structure.

Policy-gradient derivation
- For a fixed differentiable scalarization u: R^M → R, a general policy gradient can be written as ∇_θ_i J = E[ ∇_θ_i log π_i(a_i|s,p_i) · ∑_{k=1}^M ∂u/∂r_k · A_k(s,a) ], where A_k is the per-objective advantage estimate (Q_k(s,a) − V_k(s)).
- In the linear scalarization case, u(r) = w^T r with w ≥ 0, yielding ∇_θ_i J = E[ ∇_θ_i log π_i(a_i|s,p_i) · ∑_{k} w_k A_k(s,a) ].
- For non-linear scalarizations (Chebyshev, log-sum-exp, etc.), replace the inner sum with ∑_k ∂u/∂r_k · A_k(s,a). Prove local convergence to stationary points under standard assumptions (bounded rewards, Lipschitz continuity, suitable step sizes) given compatible function-approximation features.

MoMA-AC variants (design space)
- MoMA-AC-CentralCritic: centralized vector-valued critic with decentralized actors; joint TD updates, per-objective heads, and a shared representation to stabilize learning.
- MoMA-AC-FactoredCritic: per-agent critics with limited communication (attention/message passing) to approximate cross-agent Q_k terms; scalable credit assignment.
- MoMA-AC-Hypervolume: add a Pareto-front coverage term via a tractable hypervolume gradient; combine with linear or non-linear scalarizations for stability.
- MoMA-AC-Variational/Utility-Param: learn a distribution over preferences p_i and update policies to maximize expected scalarized return under the learned distribution, enabling diversity and robustness.
- Regularization and stability: PPO-like clipping or trust-region constraints, per-objective baselines, and advantage normalization.

Theoretical analysis plan
- Prove local Pareto-stationarity of fixed-preference policy updates under standard actor-critic assumptions and a finite-dimensional function class.
- Obtain sample-complexity bounds for vector-valued critics with shared representations, and provide conditions under which hypervolume-augmented objectives preserve convergence.
- Analyze non-stationarity due to other agents via a quasi-stationary approximation or by showing convergence with slowly varying opponents under appropriate learning-rate schedules.
