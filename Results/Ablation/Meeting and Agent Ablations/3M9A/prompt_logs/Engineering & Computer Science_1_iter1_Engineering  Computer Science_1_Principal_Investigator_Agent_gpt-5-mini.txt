  "Abstract": "We address the problem of transferring damage classifiers from physics-based simulations to real experimental measurements for concrete structures. We propose a self-supervised domain adaptation framework that leverages a virtual testing platform to produce large labeled synthetic coda-wave and multimodal sensor data and then adapts models to unlabeled experimental anchors using domain adversarial training, minimum class confusion loss, and BYOL-style representation learning. The main contribution is a reproducible pipeline that combines certified surrogate-generated augmentations, physics-aware pretext tasks (crack skeleton and width prediction), and calibrated uncertainty estimates to reduce sim-to-real gap. Expected outcomes are improved segmentation and classification accuracy on real concrete damage data, measurable reduction in embedding distribution distance between domains, and validated surrogate certification tests that prevent unrealistic synthetic artifacts. The approach targets reliable field-ready damage detection while minimizing the need for expensive experimental labels.",
  "Experiments": "Datasets: Pilot dataset: 200 labeled synthetic renders (multi-fidelity tiers A/B/C) + 50 paired experimental anchors with full annotations. Store datasets with DVC and fixed random seeds [42,1337,2024,7,99]. Proposed method: combined SSL + DA (domain adversarial + minimum class confusion + BYOL) with surrogate-augmented synthetic data. Metrics: segmentation IoU (primary), per-class F1, overall accuracy, Fréchet Feature Distance (FFD) between domain embeddings, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), conformal prediction coverage (90/95%), Hausdorff centerline distance (mm), fracture-energy relative error (%), topology connected-component recall (%). Statistical analysis: report bootstrap 95% CIs for primary metrics, use permutation tests for paired model comparisons, and correct for multiple comparisons with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control. Reproducibility: publish configs, Docker image, and a minimal runnable unit dataset that reproduces a pilot experiment in <1 GPU-hour with expected metrics and seed-fixed logs.",
  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Technical failure modes: surrogate models may produce unrealistic crack morphologies causing negative transfer; domain adversarial losses may collapse class separability reducing per-class accuracy. Data/evaluation biases: experimental anchors may not cover full field variability (illumination, aging, microstructure), leading to optimistic estimates of transfer. Scalability/compute limits: large-scale synthetic generation and SSL pretraining require thousands of GPU-hours; constrained budgets may force lower-fidelity renders that degrade transfer. Annotation limitations: annotator disagreement and systematic label bias (under-segmentation of thin cracks) can bias evaluation. UQ and OOD limits: ensemble and conformal methods may mis-calibrate under strong distributional shifts. Generalization bounds: results may not transfer to different sensor suites or to structures with material properties outside the simulated parameter ranges. Operational thresholds: chosen numeric gates (e.g., IoU increases, FFD reduction, ECE) may not align with all field decision costs and may require context-specific adjustment."
