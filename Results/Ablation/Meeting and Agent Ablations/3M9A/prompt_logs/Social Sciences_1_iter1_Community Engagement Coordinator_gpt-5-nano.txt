Urban Mobility Justice Researcher: "Framing and theoretical orientation
Barcelona’s climate shelters should be evaluated through a mobility justice lens that foregrounds distributional, procedural, and recognitional justice. Distributional justice asks who can physically reach shelters and with what cost; procedural justice asks who was included in siting, information design, and operations; recognitional justice asks whether the experiences, languages, cultural needs, mobility limitations, caregiving obligations and economic constraints of diverse vulnerable groups are understood and accommodated. Analytical attention must be intersectional (age, disability, gender, migration status, tenure, income, informal work) and modal (walking, bus/tram/metro, cycling, paratransit).

Priority research questions
1. Which vulnerable populations are excluded by current shelter locations and operations, and why?  
2. How does multimodal travel time to shelters vary by neighbourhood, time of day, and mobility ability?  
3. Do shelters meet accessibility standards for people with reduced mobility, sensory impairments, caregiving groups and non-Spanish/Catalan speakers?  
4. How do shelter capacity, perceived safety, and informational accessibility interact to deter or enable use among marginalized groups?  
5. What governance and participation gaps shape shelter design, communication, and trust among vulnerable residents?

Equity-focused indicators (operational)
- Spatial coverage: share (%) of population within 10-, 15-, 30-minute walking and 30-, 45-minute multimodal (walk + transit) travel-time isochrones to nearest shelter, disaggregated by vulnerability groups.  
- Vulnerability-adjusted accessibility index (VAAI): accessibility score weighted by local concentration of vulnerability (age>65, disability, low-income, non-native speakers, single-parent households).  
- Capacity adequacy ratio: shelter capacity relative to local at-risk population (beds/persons per 1,000 at-risk residents).  
- Physical accessibility compliance rate: % of shelters meeting universal design standards (ramps, wide doors, accessible toilets).  
- Information accessibility score: availability of multilingual, sensory-accessible, and SMS/analog communication channels.  
- Temporal equity metric: difference in access during peak vs off-peak hours and during anticipated climate event times.  
- Safety and gendered accessibility index: perceived safety scores from surveys disaggregated by gender and age.  
- Transport dependency gap: share of households without private vehicles outside target isochrones.  
- Wait/evacuation latency: modeled time from alert to safe shelter arrival under realistic mobility constraints.  
- Participation index: presence and degree of community engagement in siting/operational planning.

Methods and data priorities
Combine spatial network analysis (OpenStreetMap, transit GTFS, municipal shelter locations), census and vulnerability microdata, mobile/OD data for travel behavior, and climate hazard overlays (heat islands, flood risk). Complement quantitative work with qualitative methods: interviews, focus groups, participatory mapping with targeted vulnerable groups, and co-design workshops with municipal actors.

Next steps
1. Map existing shelters against vulnerability layers and compute baseline indicators. 2. Run scenario models (improved transit, pop-up shelters, targeted outreach) to test equity gains. 3.
