Central research questions
1. What are the primary sources of tin used in southern African bronzes (ca. 1200–1800 CE)?  
2. To what extent do these bronzes incorporate locally-sourced tin versus imported tin (Indian Ocean, central/West African or European)?  
3. How much do recycling and mixing practices alter isotopic/trace-element signals, and can we distinguish primary source signatures from later mixing/recycling?  
4. How do alloying and chaîne opératoire practices vary spatially and temporally and influence provenance interpretations?

Testable hypotheses
H1: The dominant tin source for southern African bronzes was imported cassiterite from Indian Ocean trade networks (e.g., Bangka/Belitung, Malay Peninsula) introduced via coastal/interior trade.  
H2: A substantial fraction of tin in many bronzes derives from recycling and mixing of multiple sources, producing isotopic heterogeneity within assemblages.  
H3: Small-scale local tin exploitation made a measurable but spatially restricted contribution to some objects.  
H4: Lead isotope ratios will be informative only for objects with appreciable Pb (deliberately added or ore-derived); low-Pb bronzes will require tin isotopes and trace elements for provenance.

Analytical workflow
