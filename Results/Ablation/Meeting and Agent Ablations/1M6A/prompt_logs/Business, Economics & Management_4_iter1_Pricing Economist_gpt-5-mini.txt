Summary definition and core metric. True price should be defined as the adjusted marginal social cost (AMSC): private marginal cost + marginal external cost (environmental, health, social) + administrative/transaction costs − any justified subsidies or negative externalities internalized elsewhere. The primary welfare metrics are (1) compensating variation (CV) and equivalent variation (EV) by household, (2) aggregate change in social welfare using a distributionally weighted sum of individual utilities, and (3) incidence measures (changes in income percentiles, poverty headcount, Gini).

Formal objectives and optimization. Frame pricing as a constrained welfare maximization problem: choose per-unit price adjustments (taxes/subsidies/tiers) to maximize social welfare W = Σi w(i) U_i(consumption_i) subject to budget balance (or specified recycling rule), political-feasibility constraints, and minimum protection constraints for vulnerable households. w(i) is a social-weight function encoding inequality aversion. First-best solution equals Pigouvian per-unit charge equal to marginal external cost; second-best solutions must incorporate market distortions, administrative feasibility, and distributional protections.

Practical pricing rules and equity safeguards. Implement Pigouvian adjustments with accompanying equity-correcting mechanisms: (a) lump-sum transfers or targeted rebates funded by revenues; (b) lifeline rates or block tariffs for essential consumption; (c) refundable tax credits for low-income households. Where behavioral frictions matter, combine price signals with nonprice interventions (information, defaults).

Empirical identification and estimation strategy. Estimate product- and context-specific marginal external costs using life-cycle assessment (LCA) linked to geospatial exposure models for environmental/health damages, and econometric estimates for social/market spillovers.
