Core concepts: Populist logic is a rhetorical and strategic repertoire that frames political-economic life as a struggle between "the people" and corrupt or out-of-touch elites, privileging direct, moralized claims about representation and authenticity. Consumption populism describes business-led deployments of that logic to shape markets, brand meaning, and consumer identity. Corporate strategy in this frame includes discursive framing (advertising, CEO speeches), performative alignment (symbolic acts, endorsements), and institutional tactics (lobbying framed as defending popular interests, bypassing intermediaries).

Mechanisms linking corporate strategy to consumption: (1) Identity activation — populist framing activates political identities that become consumption cues, driving brand preference among aligned consumers. (2) Trust reconfiguration — portraying firms as "of the people" reallocates trust from institutions to brands. (3) Polarized market segmentation — populist appeals create distinct consumer blocs and normative pressures to consume/boycott. (4) Legitimacy capture — firms use populist narratives to justify regulatory capture or market-extractive practices as populist goods.
