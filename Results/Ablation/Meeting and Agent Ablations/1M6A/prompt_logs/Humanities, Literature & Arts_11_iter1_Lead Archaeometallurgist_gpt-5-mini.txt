Summary plan and key actions to define diagnostic slag signatures and metallurgical markers for Phnom Dek

1) Sampling strategy (maximize interpretive value)
- Prioritize contextual, stratified sampling: at least 10–15 slag samples and 4–8 metal/bloom fragments per clear stratigraphic phase or pit; sample furnace wall/lining, tuyere fragments, tap slags, slag cakes and flow layers separately.

2) Analytical workflow and protocols
- Macroscopic characterization and petrology: thin section and polished sections of slags and slags/metal interfaces. Document textures (vesicularity, flow, crystallinity) and componentry.  
- Metallography: standard mounting, grinding, polishing, etch with 2–5% nital for metal microstructures. Use optical microscopy for phase distribution, inclusion morphology, and forging/heat treatment markers.  
- SEM/EDS: backscatter imaging, high-resolution mapping and point analyses for Fe, Si, Ca, Al, Mg, Mn, P, S, Ti, K, Cu, Pb. Quantify glass vs crystalline phases and locate metal prills. Use elemental maps to distinguish ore vs flux vs furnace-lining contributions.  
- XRD (powder): determine crystalline phase assemblages (fayalite, hedenbergite, kirschsteinite, melilite/gehlenite, wüstite, magnetite, spinel phases, Ca-silicates).  
- EBSD for fine-scale phase ID and crystallographic orientation where needed.  
- Quantitative chemical analyses: EPMA or LA‑ICP‑MS for trace elements (Cu, As, Ni, Pb) and to assess ore signatures; consider Pb isotope analyses to source Cu/Pb contamination and to test ore provenance.  
- Thermodynamic modeling (FactSage/Thermocalc): reconstruct liquidus, slag basicity (CaO/SiO2), FeO content and infer operating temperature and pO2 from phase assemblage and FeO/Fe ratios.

3) Diagnostic markers and interpretive criteria
- Slag basicity and CaO content: low CaO/high SiO2/Al2O3 -> unfluxed bloomery from clay-rich ore/furnace wall interaction; elevated CaO (>10–20 wt%) -> deliberate fluxing (limestone or shell).  
- FeO and fayalite abundance: high FeO and fayalite glass indicate reducing conditions and limited slag tapping; extensive crystalline fayalite + low glass suggest lower cooling rates or repeated reheating.  
- Presence of wüstite, magnetite proportions and spinel phases → constrain redox and peak temperature (wüstite indicates higher T/higher reducing conditions).  
- Phosphorus and manganese concentrations in metal and interstitial phases signal ore type (high-P ores produce characteristic P-rich phases in metal and slag).  
- Alkali (K) and Ca signals can reflect fuel ash (charcoal species) and deliberate fluxes; Cu/Ag prills indicate ore sulphide contamination or secondary smelting/cupriferous ores.  
- Textural markers: vesicular, glassy tap slags = high liquid slag fraction and tapping; dense crystalline slag laminae = slower cooling/lining collapse.
