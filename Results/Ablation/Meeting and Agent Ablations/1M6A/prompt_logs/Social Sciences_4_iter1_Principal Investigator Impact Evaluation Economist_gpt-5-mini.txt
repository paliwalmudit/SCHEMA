Primary research questions
1. Do targeted fruit vouchers increase household fruit purchase and consumption (quantity, frequency, dietary diversity) in Nigeria and Vietnam relative to a control group?  
2. Do vouchers improve affordability measured as effective price paid, share of fruit expenditure in household budget, and compensating variation/consumer surplus?  
3. What are the welfare impacts: food security, caloric/nutrient intake, subjective well-being, and substitution or crowding-out of other nutritious foods?  
4. Do vouchers generate market-level effects (price inflation or increased supply) and spillovers onto non-recipient households?  
5. What is the cost-effectiveness of vouchers per additional fruit serving, per unit increase in dietary diversity, and per DALY averted (if nutrition outcomes allow)?

Causal identification strategy (summary)
- Main approach: randomised controlled trial (RCT) with treatment assignment ensuring internal validity. Use ITT as the primary estimand and LATE (instrumental variables) to recover effects on compliers.
- Randomisation design: cluster-randomise at community/market levels to limit spillovers where feasible, and randomise intensity (low/high voucher value or share of households treated) across clusters to identify general equilibrium price effects. Within-cluster household randomisation can be used when market spillovers are unlikely.
- Stratification and blocking: stratify randomisation by baseline fruit consumption, income strata, and market access to improve balance and precision.
- Estimation: estimate outcome_it = alpha + beta*Treatment_i + X_i'gamma + delta_stratum + epsilon_it, clustering standard errors at the randomisation unit. Use assignment as an instrument for voucher redemption to estimate LATE: redemption_i = pi*assignment_i + u_i; outcome_i = tau*redemption_hat_i + v_i.
- Price and supply effects: exploit randomized saturation (variation in voucher intensity across markets) and use difference-in-differences with market fixed effects to estimate price changes over time. If feasible, randomise a supply-side treatment (small vendor subsidy for stocking fruit) to separately identify supply response.

Outcomes and measurement
- Primary outcomes: fruit purchases (voucher redemption records and receipts), intake (24-hour recalls and 7-day FFQ), affordability (price per kg/serving, fruit expenditure share, locally estimated compensating variation).  
- Secondary outcomes: household food security (FIES), dietary diversity scores, anthropometrics for children if age-appropriate, subjective well-being, labor/time use.  
- Administrative and process data: digital voucher redemption logs, vendor stock/prices collected by market surveys and mystery shoppers.

Addressing threats to identification
- Non-compliance and take-up: use encouragement design and IV.  
- Attrition: minimize with tracking, intent-to-treat analysis, and bounding methods.  
- Measurement error: combine self-reports with objective voucher and receipt data and spot-checks.  
- Spillovers: measure in neighboring households and use cluster randomisation or geographic buffers.

Analysis plan and policy relevance
- Pre-register outcomes and analysis plan, conduct power calculations for primary outcomes, and report ITT, LATE, heterogeneous effects by income, baseline intake, and market access.  
- Conduct cost-effectiveness analysis (program costs per additional serving and per nutrition outcome) and simulate scaling scenarios to inform policy decisions.
