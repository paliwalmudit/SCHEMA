Overview and research agenda

Research questions: (1) How does product differentiation across carriers on a route affect markups and profits? (2) Do different dimensions of differentiation (horizontal: schedule/route timing; vertical: cabin quality, seat pitch; multi-product: network/connectivity, loyalty programs) have distinct effects on competitive intensity and welfare? (3) How do entry, exit, and capacity/frequency adjustments change the differentiation–markup relationship?

Theoretical framing

Use a differentiated-products oligopoly framework (random‑coefficients discrete choice demand à la Berry‑Levinsohn‑Pakes) coupled with firm supply FOCs to recover marginal costs and markups. Product differentiation enters demand as distance in a multi-dimensional characteristic space (schedule/time, aircraft/amenities, fare classes, ancillary availability, connectivity). Predictions from theory:
- H1 (horizontal differentiation): Greater horizontal (time/schedule) differentiation reduces direct price competition and raises markups.
- H2 (vertical differentiation): Higher vertical quality increases willingness-to-pay and may raise prices and markups, but can increase market shares and lower elasticities; net effect on markups is ambiguous a priori and testable.
- H3 (multi-product/network effects): Greater multi-market network overlap or loyalty integration softens competition across routes and increases markups through portfolio effects.
- H4 (entry/competition shock): Exogenous increases in product similarity (entry of a similar product/provider) reduce markups; exits increase markups.

Empirical strategy

1) Demand estimation: Estimate a mixed logit (BLP) with product characteristics x_ijt (prices endogenous) to recover own/cross price elasticities and substitution patterns. Include market (origin–destination–time period) fixed effects and consumer heterogeneity.

2) Supply and markups: Use first-order conditions under Bertrand-Nash multi-product pricing (or nested pricing for alliances) to invert for marginal cost: p - mc = markup = - ( (slope matrix)^(-1) * s ) where s are market shares. Recover markups at product level and aggregate to route/firm level.

3) Identification: Instrument price with BLP-style excluded product characteristics (competitor characteristics), cost shifters (fuel price × distance, airport fees), and Hausman-style instruments (prices in other markets). Exploit plausibly exogenous schedule changes, slot reallocations, or route entry/exit as natural experiments for causal inference on differentiation.
