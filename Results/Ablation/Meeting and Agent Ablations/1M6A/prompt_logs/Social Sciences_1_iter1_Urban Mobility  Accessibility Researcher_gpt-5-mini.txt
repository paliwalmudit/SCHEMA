Below I propose a focused, methodologically rigorous way to define spatial accessibility measures and implement a multimodal spatial analysis that foregrounds mobility justice for Barcelona’s climate shelters.

Key research questions to guide analysis
- Which populations (by age, income, disability, migration status) live beyond safe walking or transit travel times to the nearest climate shelter during heat events?
- How do urban form, topography, and transport network characteristics produce systematic disparities in travel time and route safety for vulnerable groups?
- How sensitive is accessibility to disruptions (reduced transit service, closed sidewalks, heat-exposed routes) and what targeted interventions reduce inequities?

Accessibility metrics (operational definitions)
- Cumulative opportunity: proportion of vulnerable population within thresholds (walking: 10/15/20 minutes; transit: 20/30 minutes including wait and transfer; wheelchair-accessible walking: 15/20 minutes at reduced speed).
- Network travel-time to nearest shelter: multimodal shortest-path travel time on realistic networks, reported as median and 90th percentile by neighborhood and demographic group.
- Two-step floating catchment area (2SFCA) adapted for shelters: shelter capacity denominator and demand-weighted accessibility score, producing per-capita shelter accessibility.
- Gravity-based accessibility: distance-decay weighted sum of accessible shelter capacity within a 30-minute threshold to capture multiple options.
- Vulnerability-weighted accessibility index: apply weights to populations (elderly, mobility-impaired, low-income, non-native speakers) to produce an equity-focused composite score.
- Exposure-impaired route impedance: incorporate heat exposure penalties (lack of shade, surface albedo, slope) to adjust walking speeds and route desirability.

Spatial analysis approach and data
- Networks: build multimodal routable networks from OpenStreetMap (pedestrian, cycle, street network) and Barcelona GTFS for transit with time-of-day schedules. Integrate wheelchair-accessible infrastructure tags.
- Environmental layers: DEM for slope; canopy/green cover and tree-lined streets (for shade); surface materials where available; solar exposure proxies.
- Population and vulnerability: census blocks (Idescat), social vulnerability indicators, disability registers, and microdata where possible.
- Shelter attributes: coordinates, capacity, accessibility features, opening policies supplied by city sources.
- Methods: perform isochrone and shortest-path analyses at fine spatial granularity (100–250m grid), compute the metrics above, and run spatial regressions (GWR or spatial lag) to link accessibility outcomes to socio-demographics.

Interpretation within mobility justice
