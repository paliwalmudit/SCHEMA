Clinical question: In adult emergency department patients diagnosed with sepsis but without shock, does the serum anion gap (AG), and particularly the albumin-corrected anion gap (ACAG), predict an elevated serum lactate (≥2 mmol/L) at presentation, and can AG be used as a rapid bedside triage tool to prioritize lactate testing and early resuscitation?

Primary hypothesis: Elevated AG and ACAG at ED presentation independently predict serum lactate ≥2 mmol/L after adjustment for key confounders.

Recommended inclusion criteria: Adults aged ≥18 years presenting to the ED with suspected or confirmed infection and meeting Sepsis-3 criteria for sepsis (infection plus an acute change in SOFA or surrogate qSOFA criteria), who are not in shock at presentation (no persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors and MAP ≥65 mmHg without vasopressors).
