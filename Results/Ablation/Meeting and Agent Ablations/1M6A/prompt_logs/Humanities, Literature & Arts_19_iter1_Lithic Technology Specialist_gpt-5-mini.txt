Summary and goal: I propose a pragmatic, archaeologically meaningful single-test pathway that measures intrinsic hardness and stiffness from small lithic samples and translates those mechanical outputs into interpretable metrics for tool performance and use-life at Sibhudu.

Recommended single-test method and metric:
- Use instrumented micro-/nano-indentation with continuous stiffness measurement (CSM). This gives localized Vickers-equivalent hardness (H) and elastic modulus (E) from single small indents on flake surfaces, with very small sample preparation and the ability to detect cracking during/after indentation.
- Derive two simple ratios from the indentation outputs that are known predictors of wear and fracture behavior in brittle materials: H/E (indicator of elastic strain tolerance and capacity to sustain a sharp edge) and H^3/E^2 (a proxy for resistance to plastic deformation and initial edge retention). Combine these with an indentation-derived fracture toughness estimate (K_IC) when radial cracks are observable.
- Define a composite Lithic Performance Index (LPI) = normalized(H^3/E^2) × normalized(K_IC). LPI can be calculated from the single indentation test (H, E, cracking) and scaled 0–1 for comparison across materials.

Sampling strategy for Sibhudu:
- Sample the main raw materials represented in the assemblage (silcrete, chert/flint, quartz/quartzite, other siliceous rocks), including both cortex and interior, and orientations relative to bedding/grain where relevant.
- For each material-source cluster aim for n ≥ 25–30 indents on independent specimens to capture intra-source variability (weathering, grain size, heterogeneity).
- Include archaeological flakes/blanks when available to compare “as-used” surfaces with fresh raw material.

Validation and performance experiments:
- Controlled knapping to produce standardized edges (same platform angle, edge thickness) using a single experienced knapper, then measure initial LPI on flake edges.
- Edge-retention tests: standardized cutting/scraping cycles on a consistent substrate (e.g., hide, plant fiber, soft wood) with quantitative counts to failure (loss of function or catastrophic chipping), measuring edge angle and micro-edge damage after set intervals with SEM/light profilometry.
- Correlate LPI and its components (H, E, K_IC, H/E, H^3/E^2) with: number of effective strokes to failure, dominant wear mode (abrasive dulling vs. chipping), and resharpening intervals.
- Include artificial flawing and accelerated weathering experiments to quantify sensitivity of the single-test metric to microfractures and surface alteration.
