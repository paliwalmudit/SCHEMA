Summary and classification: Analytical errors in a general hospital laboratory typically include instrument malfunction (mechanical or electronic), calibration and linearity failures, reagent degradation or lot-to-lot variability, poor temperature or light control, carryover, interference (hemolysis, lipemia, icterus, drugs), pipetting or dilution errors, software/middleware processing errors, and failures in post-analytical verification (transcription, reference range misapplication). These must be distinguished from pre-analytical and post-analytical problems because remediation differs.

Clinical impact and risk stratification: The clinical consequences range from minor (repeat testing, delay) to severe (misdiagnosis, inappropriate therapy, transfusion error, failure to detect life-threatening electrolyte or coagulation abnormalities). Prioritize corrective actions by patient-safety risk: highest priority are critical-care analytes (K+, troponin, glucose, INR, blood gases), then high-volume routine tests (CBC, electrolytes, creatinine), and lastly low-volume specialized tests. Consider both immediate harm potential and frequency when assigning remediation priority.

Feasible corrective actions with patient-safety relevance:
- Strengthen daily internal QC with documented Westgard/Levy-Jennings monitoring and predefined stop-rules for critical analytes; ensure immediate investigation and repeat testing before releasing critical results that fail QC.
- Implement strict reagent and lot verification procedures and maintain controlled storage conditions with logs and alarms; require lot-to-lot comparability testing for critical assays.
- Institute scheduled preventive maintenance and verification checks after service or power events; keep spare parts/backup analyzers for 24/7 critical tests.
