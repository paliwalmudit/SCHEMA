Social Cohesion Theorist: "Proposed theoretical definition:
Social cohesion is the dynamic quality of a society that reflects the strength and quality of connections among people and between people and institutions, expressed through mutual trust, shared identity and values, inclusive norms of reciprocity and cooperation, and effective, legitimate mechanisms for conflict resolution and resource distribution. In African contexts, social cohesion must explicitly incorporate the roles of ethnic and kinship ties, customary institutions, religious organizations, and informal economic networks, as well as patterns of inequality, mobility, and state presence.

Core measurable dimensions (conceptual mapping to indicators):
1. Horizontal trust and reciprocity: measured by survey items on trust in neighbors and co-ethnics, frequency of mutual aid, willingness to cooperate in community projects, and network density of support ties (social network analysis).
2. Bridging/cohesive cross-group relations: measured by interethnic friendship rates, participation in mixed-group associations, social distance scales, and rates of intergroup marriage or shared organizations.
3. Linking trust and institutional legitimacy: measured by trust in local authorities, police, courts and central government; perceptions of fairness and corruption; and legitimacy of customary versus state institutions.
4. Inclusive participation and civic engagement: measured by voter turnout, attendance at public meetings, membership in civic associations, rates of collective action, and gender/youth participation disaggregated.
5. Perceived and objective equality of access: measured by perceived discrimination scales, reported unequal treatment, Gini coefficient, access to basic services (water, health, education) at local level, and land tenure security.
6. Conflict absence and dispute resolution capacity: measured by incidence and severity of local violence (UCDP, ACLED), frequency and satisfaction with dispute resolution mechanisms, and local policing or customary adjudication effectiveness.
7. Shared identity and normative consensus: measured by national versus local identity strength, agreement on core norms (e.g., tolerance, civic duties), and attitudes toward pluralism.

Hypothesized relationships:
- Higher levels of bridging ties and linking trust reduce the probability of intergroup conflict and increase public service responsiveness.
- High inequality and perceived exclusion weaken horizontal trust and participation, producing fragile or negative cohesion.
- Strong customary institutions can compensate for weak state legitimacy when inclusive and accountable, but may undermine cohesion if exclusionary.
- Youth unemployment and rapid urbanization strain reciprocity norms and reduce community-level cooperation unless offset by inclusive institutions.

Measurement strategy and validation:
