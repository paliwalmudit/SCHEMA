Key gaps and research priorities
1. Diagnostic validity against gold standards: Few studies evaluate LLM outputs against structured clinical interviews (SCID, MINI). Prospective diagnostic accuracy studies are needed with blinded comparison to trained clinicians using DSM-5/ICD-11 criteria.
2. Safety for high-risk presentations: Suicidality, acute psychosis, mania, and severe self-harm require explicit safety testing; current work often omits simulated crisis scenarios and harm-rate metrics.
3. Calibration and uncertainty estimation: LLMs rarely report confidence calibrated to clinical risk. Studies should assess calibration, show thresholds for action, and evaluate miscalibration harms.
4. Population representativeness and fairness: Training/evaluation datasets underrepresent many demographic groups and clinical presentations. Systematic bias audits across age, sex, race/ethnicity, socioeconomic status, and language are essential.
5. Human-in-the-loop workflows and escalation: There is limited evidence on optimal clinician–LLM collaboration models (triage, decision support, psychotherapy augmentation). Comparative trials of workflow models are needed.
6. Explainability and clinical reasoning: LLM rationale often lacks clinically meaningful chain-of-thought that maps to diagnostic criteria or differential diagnosis; evaluation should test whether explanations align with accepted psychiatric reasoning.

Concrete evaluation criteria (prioritized)
1. Clinical accuracy: sensitivity/specificity, PPV/NPV for diagnoses versus structured interview gold standard.
2. Safety-critical error rates: false negatives for suicidality/psychosis, time-to-detection for crisis cues.
3. Calibration: Brier score, calibration plots, and threshold-specific decision metrics.
4. Fairness: subgroup performance disparities, equalized odds, demographic parity where appropriate.
5. Reliability: test–retest stability and inter-rater concordance with clinicians (kappa).
6. Clinical usefulness: decision curve analysis, number needed to treat/harm, and user acceptability in clinicians and patients.
7. Explainability fidelity: clinician-rated usefulness of model explanations and alignment with diagnostic criteria.
