Thank you — below I outline a focused qualitative research plan and key gaps to fill that align with our project question: Could AI technologies be harnessed to break down barriers to inclusivity for women’s entrepreneurship in tourism?

1. Core social, cultural, and structural barriers to investigate
- Gendered time burdens and care responsibilities restricting mobility and online availability.  
- Norms limiting women’s public presence, networking, negotiation and access to finance.  
- Low digital literacy, limited device access, and language barriers.  
- Safety and reputational risks associated with visibility on digital platforms.  
- Algorithmic bias in platforms (e.g., visibility, recommendation algorithms) replicating exclusion.  
- Regulatory, financial and infrastructure constraints (payments, connectivity, licensing).  
- Intersectional disadvantages (age, ethnicity/caste, disability, migration status, marital status).

2. Priority research questions (qualitative + participatory)
- How do women entrepreneurs in different tourism niches perceive AI-based tools (recommendation systems, chatbots, pricing tools, translation, safety features)?  
- Which social norms and household dynamics enable or block women’s adoption of AI-enabled services?  
- Where do algorithmic practices reproduce or mitigate gendered exclusion (visibility, trust signals, reviews)?  
- What safety, privacy, and reputational concerns do women have about AI-mediated visibility and data use?  
- What design features, training formats, and governance mechanisms would women identify as necessary for adoption and benefit?  
- How do intersectional identities shape needs and responses to AI interventions?

3. Methods and sampling
- Mixed qualitative methods: in-depth interviews, focus groups (segmented by age/ethnicity), life histories, participant observation at workplaces, and photovoice/digital diaries to capture daily constraints.  
- Participatory co-design workshops and low-fi prototyping/usability testing of AI tool concepts to surface preferences and unintended harms.  
- Key informant interviews with male household members, platform operators, local tourism associations, microfinance agents, and local regulators.  
- Purposive maximum-variation sampling to include rural/urban, formal/informal entrepreneurs, migrant and disabled women.

4. Culturally sensitive outcome measures
- Co-created indicators of agency and wellbeing (e.g., perceived control over bookings, ability to set prices, mobility freedoms).  
- Time-use changes (objective diary + recall) and safety incidents reported.  
- Economic indicators: bookings, revenue diversity, digital payment uptake.  
- Digital capability metrics: language access, device access, confidence using tools.  
- Trust and privacy perceptions, and perceived fairness of platform visibility. Use validated empowerment scales adapted via participatory translation and piloting.

5. Ethics and risks
- Prioritize informed consent, confidentiality, secure handling of digital traces, and steps to avoid increasing reputational/safety risks. Compensate participants fairly.

6. Evaluation suggestions
- Develop a gendered Theory of Change linking AI features to outcomes, then pilot iterative co-designed prototypes with process evaluation, qualitative longitudinal follow-up, and algorithmic audits for disparate impacts.
