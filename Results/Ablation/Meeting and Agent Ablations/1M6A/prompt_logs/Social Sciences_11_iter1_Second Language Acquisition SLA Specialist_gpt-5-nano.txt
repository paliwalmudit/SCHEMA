Analytic framework (steps and coding dimensions)
1. Identification: locate candidate repair sequences by verbal markers (e.g., huh, what, sorry, you mean), pauses (>0.2s), overlaps bracketed by mismatch or trouble tokens, and visible embodied cues (gaze shift, pen-up) marking trouble.
2. Categorization (core repair taxonomy adapted from Sacks/Jefferson/Schegloff):
   - Self-initiated self-repair (SISR): speaker detects trouble and repairs own prior turn.
   - Self-initiated other-repair (SIOR): speaker signals trouble and invites others to repair.
   - Other-initiated self-repair (OISR): recipient initiates repair and original speaker repairs.
   - Other-initiated other-repair (OIOR): recipient initiates and completes repair on behalf of original speaker.
3. Repair-initiator form (subtypes):
   - Open-class (open request: “Huh?”, “What?”).
   - Restricted-class (more constrained: “Which one?”, “Who?”).
   - Specific/other-correction (explicit candidate: “Do you mean X?”; recast).
   - Confirmation check (tag questions, rising intonation).
4. Sequential/contextual coding:
   - Placement relative to turn (immediate, delayed, second-position).
   - Relation to classroom activity type (initiation-response-feedback sequence, pair work, whole-class).
   - Authority axis (teacher-initiated vs. peer-initiated) and its interactional consequences.
5. Multimodal and outcome coding:
   - Embodied conduct (gaze, pointing, posture).
   - Repair outcome: uptake (full/partial/none), learning action (expansion, practice), repair abandonment.
