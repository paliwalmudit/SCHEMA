Key archaeological performance metrics the test must predict
- Flake predictability: propensity for controlled fracture propagation (successful platform formation, low step/hinge termination rates).

- Fracture reliability under impact/force: susceptibility to catastrophic failure, linked to fracture toughness.
- Workability trade-offs: hardness vs toughness vs stiffness influences on knapping effort and suitability for specific tool forms (points, blades, scrapers).
- Sensitivity to heat/weathering: whether small thermal or weathering changes significantly alter the above behaviors.

Recommended single-test approach (practical, diagnostic, minimally destructive)
- A combined instrumented microindentation protocol on a small, cortex-free sample that yields: Vickers hardness (HV), elastic modulus (E, from unloading stiffness), and fracture toughness (K_IC) by measuring indentation-induced crack lengths (radial cracks, Palmqvist or median crack methods). Augment with ultrasonic pulse velocity on the same specimen to refine E and detect anisotropy and microcracks.
- Rationale: instrumented indentation gives hardness and modulus from one apparatus; crack-length-based toughness is routinely measurable on siliceous and fine-grained lithologies; ultrasonic provides independent stiffness and detects internal flaws.
