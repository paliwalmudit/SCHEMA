As Clinical Lead in Orthopedics & Thrombosis, I recommend the motivation scale be explicitly mapped to clinical constructs that drive adherence and to measurable endpoints that matter for patient safety and outcomes.

Core constructs the scale should capture:
- Perceived susceptibility and seriousness of postoperative VTE. 
- Perceived benefits and barriers to prophylaxis (efficacy beliefs, side‑effect concerns). 
- Self‑efficacy for correct injection or oral medication use and for obtaining/refilling medications. 
- Practical barriers: regimen complexity, cost/insurance, transport/pharmacy access. 
- Social and care‑team support, including caregiver involvement. 
- Quality and clarity of discharge education and provider communication. 
- Physical capacity (mobility, pain) and cognitive factors (health literacy, depression). 
- Prior VTE history and personal experience with anticoagulation.

Priority clinical and process endpoints for validation:
- Adherence measured objectively (electronic injection/medication monitors, pill counts, pharmacy refill data) and by validated self‑report. 
- Duration of prophylaxis completed versus prescribed. 
- Safety endpoints: symptomatic DVT/PE, major bleeding (ISTH definition), wound complications, and 30‑ and 90‑day readmissions. 
- Patient‑reported outcomes: knowledge, satisfaction, and intention to adhere. 
- Process outcomes: successful follow‑up contact, completion of education, and intervention uptake.

Item generation and phrasing guidance:
- Use simple, actionable language and a 5‑point Likert response for most items. Example items: “I believe I am at risk of a blood clot after my surgery”; “I am confident I can give myself injections for the prescribed duration”; “I am worried about bleeding from the anticoagulant”; “I can afford and access my anticoagulant.” Include caregiver‑directed items when relevant.

Recruitment and safety monitoring recommendations:
- Enroll a representative sample across hip and knee arthroplasty, anticoagulant types (LMWH, DOACs, aspirin where used), ages, and socioeconomic strata; stratify and power for subgroup analyses. Exclude patients with contraindications to prophylaxis where adherence is moot. Implement active safety monitoring with rapid reporting of VTE and major bleeding, linkage to EHR, and a study safety officer or DSMB, with pre‑specified stop/reporting rules.

Validation and interpretation:
- Follow best practices: content validity via expert panels and cognitive interviews, pilot testing, exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses, internal consistency (alpha/omega), test–retest reliability, and criterion validity against objective adherence measures. Define clinically meaningful thresholds (e.g., scale cutoffs that predict <80% adherence) and interpret effect sizes in relation to expected changes in adherence and downstream VTE risk while acknowledging that behavioral change is a mediator, not a direct guarantee of fewer events.

Implementation suggestions:
- Create both a brief clinical version for discharge workflows and a longer research version. Integrate into EMR with automated flags for low‑motivation patients to trigger targeted interventions (education reinforcement, pharmacy outreach, home nursing, or reminder systems).

Research gaps to pursue:
- Validation across languages/cultures, real‑world implementation trials testing targeted interventions guided by the scale, cost‑effectiveness analyses, and use of objective digital adherence tools to refine predictive performance.
