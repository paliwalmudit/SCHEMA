Conditioning allows a single neural policy per agent to represent many trade-offs (the Pareto front). For actor updates we support two modes: efficient scalarized gradients (sample a preference and optimize its weighted sum) and Pareto-aware multi-gradient combination (MGDA) that finds updates that improve all objectives where possible. The main contribution is an integrated, testable design that encodes Pareto trade-offs in single-agent policies for continuous multi-agent control and exposes the cost–quality trade-offs between scalarization and MGDA. Expected outcome: a reproducible method that improves Pareto-front coverage and preference-response without training separate policies for each weight.",

  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Technical failure modes: centralized joint critics scale poorly with agent count (joint-action dimension explosion) and may suffer high variance or bias from off-policy replay and target networks (biased value estimates). Computational costs: MGDA requires storing K gradient vectors (O(K·|θ|) memory) and solving a QP per update (worst-case O(K^3) time), which can be prohibitive for large K; solver overhead may dominate runtime. Approximation limits: neural function approximation error (ε_approx) depends on model size and budget; large ε_approx degrades stationarity and Pareto quality. Preference identifiability: with limited or noisy human queries the posterior over preferences may not contract, producing biased policies. Human-in-the-loop constraints: user fatigue and inconsistent responses limit usable query budgets. Reproducibility: GPU nondeterminism (CuDNN, mixed precision) can cause run variance; exact bitwise reproducibility may be infeasible on all hardware. Generalization: trained policies may not transfer to tasks with different dynamics or objective trade-offs beyond the training distribution.
