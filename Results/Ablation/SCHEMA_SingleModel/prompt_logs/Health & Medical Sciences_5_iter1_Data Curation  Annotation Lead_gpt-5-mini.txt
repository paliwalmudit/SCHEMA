3) Wavelet-derived features for memory and decoder
- For each high-frequency subband compute local-energy maps (sum of squared coefficients within small windows) and oriented magnitude responses; these are robust boundary indicators less sensitive to speckle-phase variability than raw coefficients.

- Boundary-aware loss: weight boundary pixels (derived from H-energy) higher via boundary IoU or Hausdorff surrogate.
- Reconstruction loss on feature maps: L1 loss between encoder features and inverse-DWT(L+H) to ensure wavelet decompositions retain salient info.
- Contrastive or retrieval loss on keys to encourage discriminative memory entries across long-term anatomical states.

6) Evaluation and ablations
- Metrics: Dice/IoU, boundary F1, temporal stability (mean IoU over frame gaps, boundary flicker measured as boundary position variance), and FPS/memory footprint.
- Ablations: wavelet family (Haar, Daubechies, Symlet, DT-CWT), decomposition levels (1–4), energy vs full coefficients, separate vs unified L/H memories, key compression level, top-k read sizes.
