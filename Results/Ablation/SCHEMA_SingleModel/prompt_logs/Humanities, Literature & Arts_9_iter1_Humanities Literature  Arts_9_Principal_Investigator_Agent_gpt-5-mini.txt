  "Related Work": "Prior work on ethics-review practices and privacy in non-medical research emphasizes privacy risk, threat modeling, synthetic data, operational security, and responsible transparency. Limitations of existing approaches include: (1) few studies combine a reproducible qualitative coding method with quantitative prediction of decisions; (2) automated topic models capture lexical themes but lack the fine-grained, theory-driven codes that SCAT provides; (3) prior empirical privacy work often proposes anonymization targets without validating them against actual committee reasoning in comments; (4) reproducibility and red-team protocols for re‑identification risk are under-specified; (5) most analyses focus on medical or English-language contexts, limiting transferability to Japanese non-medical REC practice.",
  "Abstract": "This study analyzes research‑ethics committee (REC) review comments on non‑medical research proposals at a Japanese university using the SCAT (Steps for Coding and Theorization) qualitative method and tests whether SCAT-derived thematic codes predict REC decisions. We extract structured codes from reviewer comments, map codes to measurable features, and train simple classifiers to evaluate predictability of outcomes (administrative approval, conditional approval, revision required, rejection). Main contribution: a reproducible pipeline that links rigorous qualitative SCAT coding to quantitative prediction and operational metrics, producing an interpretable codebook of salient ethical concerns (e.g., privacy, threat models, synthetic data, operational security) and a predictive evaluation of their decision impact. Expected outcome: (1) a validated SCAT codebook for REC comments in Japanese non-medical contexts; (2) evidence about which ethical themes most strongly influence decisions; (3) an artifact set (codebook, annotated dataset, analysis code) to inform REC guidance and empirical anonymization policies. The work supports evidence-based REC practice and clearer institutional recommendations.",
  "Experiments": "Datasets: collect all REC review comments and metadata for non-medical proposals at a Japanese university from 2016–2023 (target N = 1,200–1,800 records). Create a synthetic mirror dataset with same distributions for open release. Annotation: two trained coders apply SCAT to a random stratified sample of 600 records to produce a codebook; measure inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa) on codes (goal kappa ≥0.7). Manually resolve disagreements and produce final labeled set of 1,200 SCAT-code annotations via coder + single-pass adjudication. Baselines: (A) lexical TF-IDF features + logistic regression; (B) LDA topic model (k=20) + logistic regression on topic proportions; (C) SCAT-coded binary features + logistic regression; (D) combined features (SCAT + TF-IDF) + logistic regression; (E) random forest on combined features. Evaluation metrics: classification accuracy, macro F1, precision/recall per class, ROC AUC for one-vs-rest, calibration (Brier score). Clustering metrics where applicable: normalized mutual information (NMI) and cluster purity. Experimental setup: stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test split by decision label and data type.   "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Single-institution sample may not generalize to other Japanese universities or international RECs. REC comments are subjective and may reflect reviewer style rather than consistent policy reasoning. SCAT coding is interpretive; coder bias and imperfect inter-rater agreement can limit reproducibility. Class imbalance (rare rejections) can reduce reliability of performance estimates for some outcomes. Language-specific preprocessing (Japanese tokenization and stopword lists) may affect automated baselines differently than SCAT human codes. Legal and privacy constraints may limit sharing of raw comments; synthetic mirroring may not capture all linguistic nuances. Computational limits are modest for classifiers but DP-trained large language models or DP-SGD experiments (if attempted later) will require substantial GPU resources. Results tie codes to correlation with decisions, not causal proof of committee intent; predictive association does not prove reviewers would change decisions under different information."
