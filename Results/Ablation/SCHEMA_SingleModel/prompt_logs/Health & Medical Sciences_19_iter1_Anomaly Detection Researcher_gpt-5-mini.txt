Key LM-based features and how to compute them
- Token surprisal (autoregressive LM): length-normalized sum of negative log-probabilities per token from a GPT-style model; produce per-token and sentence aggregates and z-score them against a clean-corpus distribution to expose unusual lexical choices or factual contradictions.  
- MLM pseudo-loglikelihood (PLM): compute pseudo-loglikelihood by masking each token in turn and summing negative log-probabilities; this highlights improbable tokens in context while mitigating subword fragmentation issues.  
- Likelihood-ratio score: compute log p_orig(x) − log p_ref(x) where p_ref is a general-domain LM and p_orig is a domain-adapted LM; this reduces false positives from domain-specific phrasing.  
- Reconstruction loss (denoising autoencoder): train a Transformer denoiser on clean data; use token-level and sentence-level reconstruction error as anomaly scores for incoherence and deletion/insertion errors.  
- Embedding anomaly distances: compute contextual sentence embeddings (SBERT or fine-tuned contrastive encoder) and score by Mahalanobis distance or kNN distance to a clean embedding bank to surface semantic outliers.  
- Syntactic surprisal: probability of POS/tag sequence under a POS n-gram/LM; useful for grammatical agreement and punctuation anomalies.  
- Surface/character signals: character n-gram TF-IDF, edit-distance to nearest clean token, and OCR/confusion-matrix likelihoods for typographic errors.
