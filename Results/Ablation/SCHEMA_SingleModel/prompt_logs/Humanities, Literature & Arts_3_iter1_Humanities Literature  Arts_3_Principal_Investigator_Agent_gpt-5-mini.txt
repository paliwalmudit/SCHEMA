Primary quantitative outcomes are change in ethnic identity (MEIM) and Sense of Community Index; secondary outcomes include state affect (short PANAS), meaning in life (MLQ), well-being (WHO-5), archival engagement logs, and optional biosensor-derived autonomic markers (HRV). We pair multilevel longitudinal models (three-level: time within person within household) and pre-registered mediation tests with qualitative thematic analysis and interrater reliability. Contributions: (1) a reproducible quasi-experimental protocol for archive exposure, (2) a psychometric pipeline for cross-language validation, (3) seeded Monte Carlo power simulations, and (4) an evaluation of affective metadata and privacy-engineering trade-offs. Expected outcome: clear, reproducible evidence about the archive's social and affective effects and operational guidance for affective metadata and ethical data sharing.",
  "Experiments": "Datasets: (a) archive object metadata and digital surrogates; (b) August 2024 oral-history interviews (existing); (c) pilot cohort N=60 stratified by generation and donor status; (d) scale cohort N=150–200. Include optional biosensor subset N≈30 for physiological signals. Baselines: (1) no-access control (waitlist), (2) generic-access control (same materials but without affective metadata and without mediated community liaison), (3) matched non-random control via propensity scores when randomisation is not possible. Exposure operationalisation: scripted mediated encounter (dose measured as minutes of mediated session and number of items viewed); timing: single mediated session between baseline and immediate follow-up; repeated exposure logged for longitudinal dose-response. Timepoints: T0 baseline, T1 immediate post-exposure (0–7 days), T2 3 months, T3 12 months. Evaluation metrics: primary — MEIM change (ethnic identity salience) and Sense of Community Index change; secondary — PANAS short state change, MLQ presence change, WHO-5 change, archival engagement metrics (session counts, time on item), qualitative code frequencies and thematic saturation, interrater Cohen's kappa for coding, metadata completeness (% fields populated), middleware enforcement success rate (% requests correctly authorized). Biosensor metrics: mean HR, RMSSD (HRV measure), phasic skin conductance response counts. Statistical analysis: three-level multilevel growth models (time|participant|household), fixed effects for time, exposure, time×exposure, generation, donor status; random intercepts at household and participant and random slope for time at participant. Inference sets: intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol contrasts. Pre-registered comparisons: full intervention vs generic-access vs waitlist. Ablation studies: (A) affective metadata removed (same mediated contact) vs full; (B) mediated liaison removed vs full; (C) biosensor subset vs no-biosensor. Power and reproducibility: publish seeded Monte Carlo simulation code and spec sheet with parameter values (effect sizes, ICCs, residual variances, attrition) and exact random seed. Missing data and sensitivity: pre-specify pattern-mixture and selection models for MNAR checks and report primary estimates with sensitivity bounds. Multiple testing: primary outcomes limited to two pre-registered contrasts; secondary analyses controlled by Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate. Implementation details: publish analysis scripts, seed, and CI/CD repository before data collection; provide exact variable definitions and preprocessing steps for physiological and transcript data.",
  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Technical failures: middleware or consent flag enforcement bugs may misapply access rules; biosensor data may be noisy or non-usable due to artifacts. Data and evaluation biases: selection bias if donors and participants are unrepresentative; differential attrition across groups may bias longitudinal estimates. Psychometrics: measurement invariance across generations and languages may fail, making pooled comparisons invalid. Privacy constraints: strict GDPR or Article 9 interpretations may limit sample sizes or available variables; differential privacy or k-anonymity may reduce data utility for small cells. Scalability and compute: differential privacy pipelines and Monte Carlo sims can be computationally expensive for repeated re-runs. Generalization limits: findings will be specific to the German minority context in Silesia and may not transfer to other minority archives. Biosensor limits: small biosensor sample reduces power for physiological claims; HRV and EDA are sensitive to movement and recording conditions, producing artifacts. Ethical constraints: archival preservation obligations may prevent full deletion on revocation, which can affect participant trust and retention."
