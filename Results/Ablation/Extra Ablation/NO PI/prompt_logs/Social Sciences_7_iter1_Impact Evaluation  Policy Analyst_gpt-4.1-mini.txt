To reconcile feasibility in target geographies, a phased stepped-wedge design across 4–6 pilot sites with strong local partner networks will enable adequate statistical power while accommodating recruitment constraints. Detailed recruitment and retention plans will be co-developed with community liaisons and women’s business associations to ensure representative sampling and minimize attrition.

Second, our analytic plan will pre-specify estimands centered on intention-to-treat effects for key outcomes such as visibility lift, booking conversions, revenue growth, and loan approvals, supplemented by secondary and mediation analyses (e.g., impressions as a mediator). We will employ covariate-adjusted regression models with robust standard errors, clustering correction, multiple hypothesis testing corrections (Benjamini-Hochberg), and implement multiple imputation to address missing data under MAR assumptions, augmented by sensitivity analyses for MNAR mechanisms. Exploratory intersectional subgroup analyses will include strata by ethnicity, rural/urban status, business stage, and literacy levels, with clear distinction between prespecified confirmatory and exploratory tests to mitigate p-hacking risks.

Third, we will operationalize “visibility” as unique, deduplicated impressions per 1,000 distinct user sessions within a rolling 30-day window, employing rigorous server log filtering to exclude bots and reduce measurement noise. This metric will be logged with rank-weighted impression scores, standardized sampling rules, timestamp normalization, and transparent audit trails to ensure reproducibility and accountability.

Fourth, privacy protections will be concretely implemented through differential privacy mechanisms with utility-loss tradeoff analyses across ε-values ranging from 0.1 to 4. We will publish utility curves (e.g., AUC, CTR, revenue impacts) and disclosure risk assessments for synthetic datasets. Federated learning feasibility will be assessed for connectivity-constrained contexts, emphasizing secure aggregation, update compression, and asynchronous syncing, alongside fail-safe centralized architectures with strict DP and secure enclave protections to ensure robust privacy guarantees.

Fifth, the monitoring framework will feature a standardized, disaggregated KPI dashboard accessible to technical teams, oversight bodies, and participating entrepreneurs. Automated alerts will trigger human review upon surpassing fairness thresholds—such as subgroup visibility decline >10%, false negative rate disparities >5 percentage points, or sudden recourse request spikes—enabling timely interventions. Monthly adversarial and gaming tests with escalation protocols will safeguard system integrity. Importantly, monitoring will embed capacity-building resources to empower local partners in ongoing oversight and recourse handling.

Sixth, our cost-effectiveness analysis will integrate direct development and operational costs with projected economic benefits—booking increments, revenue gains, and loan approvals—to provide policymakers actionable ROI estimates. Sensitivity analyses will explore economic viability under diverse market conditions and intervention scales, informing scalable funding and partnership models.

Finally, we will synthesize qualitative insights through participatory evaluation with diverse women entrepreneurs, capturing user experiences, trust dynamics, and contextual barriers that augment quantitative evidence. These insights will guide iterative refinement of AI models, UX design, and governance frameworks, ensuring culturally grounded, empowering, and sustainable interventions.

Immediate next steps include finalizing power analyses and MDE tables, completing the statistical analysis plan, operationalizing measurement definitions with engineering partners, publishing DP utility curves and federated feasibility reports, designing the participatory monitoring dashboard, and framing cost-effectiveness models tied to local market data. Close collaboration with local stakeholders will remain essential to align scientific rigor with operational realism and policy relevance.

Through this integrated framework, we will robustly quantify and optimize the effectiveness of AI technologies in breaking down inclusivity barriers, generating actionable evidence to support equitable and sustainable empowerment of women entrepreneurs in tourism ecosystems.
