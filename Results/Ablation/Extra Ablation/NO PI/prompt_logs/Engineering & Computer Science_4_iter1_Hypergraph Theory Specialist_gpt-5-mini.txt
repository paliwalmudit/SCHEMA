I propose a principled set of hypergraph constructions, weighting and normalization rules, and spectral operators to use as the backbone of a state-space hypergraph neural model for node classification. These choices prioritize preservation of higher-order structure, numerical stability for state-space propagation, and compatibility with learnable weighting.

1) Basic objects and matrices.
- Represent the hypergraph as H = (V,E,w) with incidence matrix B in {0,1}^{|V|x|E|}, where B(v,e)=1 iff v in e, and hyperedge weight vector w(e)>0 collected into diagonal W.
- Vertex degree matrix Dv where Dv(v,v)=sum_e B(v,e) w(e). Hyperedge degree matrix De where De(e,e)=|e| (or sum_v B(v,e)).

2) Two canonical expansions and their tradeoffs.
- Bipartite (incidence) representation: preserves full higher-order information and is used directly in message passing between vertices and hyperedges.
- Clique expansion (pairwise): adjacency A_clique = B W S B^T - diag where S scales intra-edge pairwise weight; typical choice S(e)=1/(|e|-1). Clique expansion reduces expressivity and can introduce dense edges for large hyperedges, so use only when pairwise modeling is required.

3) Recommended normalized operators (for spectral / state-space use).
- Random-walk shift operator: S_rw = Dv^{-1} B W De^{-1} B^T. This maps vertex signals to smoothed vertex signals via hyperedges and preserves probability mass (useful for autoregressive dynamics).
- Symmetric normalized shift (symmetric diffusion): S_sym = Dv^{-1/2} B W De^{-1} B^T Dv^{-1/2}. This operator is symmetric positive semi-definite after subtracting from identity and yields the normalized Laplacian:
  L = I - S_sym.
These operators retain higher-order relations encoded by B and are numerically stable when Dv and De are regularized (add epsilon).

4) Weighting schemes.
- Default: w(e)=1/|e| (penalizes large hyperedges).
- Frequency-informed: w(e) ∝ log(1 + freq(e)) or TF-IDF style when hyperedges are contexts.
- Feature-aware learnable weights: w(e)=softplus(MLP(mean_{v in e} x_v)) to allow end-to-end adaptation; implement weight normalization (scale to maintain spectral radius <=1).
- Label-aware reweighting for semi-supervised tasks: amplify hyperedges with high label purity.

5) Practical normalization and stability rules for state-space models.
- Normalize W so that spectral radius rho(S) ≤ 1 (scale W by 1/max(1,rho_est)); use S_rw for guaranteed stochastic propagation.
- Add self-loop: S' = alpha I + (1-alpha) S to control memory and ensure contractivity.
- Use residual connections and layer-wise normalization to prevent vanishing/exploding state updates.
