Key mineralogical and microstructural controls to quantify
- Modal mineralogy (vol.%): relative abundance of hard phases (quartz, feldspar, certain heavy minerals) versus soft phases (clays, carbonates, sericitic alteration products).

- Porosity and pore geometry: total porosity, pore throat size and connectivity strongly reduce stiffness and apparent hardness.  
- Microcrack density and orientation: pre-existing microfractures reduce both stiffness and toughness and introduce anisotropy.  
- Clay content/type and degree of alteration: clays and weathering tuffs dramatically lower mechanical properties.  
- Crystallographic preferred orientation (CPO): preferred alignment of anisotropic minerals (e.g., micas) produces directional mechanical behavior.  
- Intragranular inclusions and lattice defects: affect local hardness measured by indentation.
