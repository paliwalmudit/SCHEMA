- Implement Monte Carlo simulations to quantify power for single‑grain OSL age distinctions between adjacent stratigraphic units, varying dose‑response, overdispersion, and grain yield per sample, to determine minimum grains measured and accepted for 80–95% power. 
- For refitting, power analyses should model detectable differences between 3% and 8–10% refit rates, given observed artifact counts per horizon; provide explicit sample‑size tables (e.g., artifacts per horizon, number of horizons) to justify target counts. 
- For biomolecular screening, develop binomial power curves across plausible success rates (5–30%), with confidence intervals and decision thresholds for continuing or reallocating effort.

2) QA/QC SOPs by context
- Standardize nested wet‑sieving arrays (10 mm, 4 mm, 2 mm, 0.5 mm) with context‑specific adjustments (e.g., finer screens in silts with microdebitage preservation; coarser screens where knapping floors dominate). Include a 0.25–0.5 mm micro‑fraction for targeted microdebitage when preservation permits. 
- Specify in situ dose‑rate sampling: radiometric measurements (gamma spectroscopy) on representative bed‑sediments, paired with U, Th, K concentrations by ICP‑MS; document geometry, sample mass, and water‑content correction factors with uncertainty propagation. 
- Water‑content correction protocol: collect paired moisture measurements across facies and incorporate a calibration curve to apply site‑specific corrections with uncertainty bounds.

3) Micromorphology decision tree
- Define explicit criteria for “eligible for destructive biomolecular sampling”: intact, continuous lamination with minimal bioturbation; presence of primary depositional features; positive refit context linkage; micromorphology showing limited pore‑water disturbance in overlying horizons. 
- Specify rejection rules: pervasive reworking indicators, mixed grain populations with disorderly stratigraphy, or lack of stratigraphic continuity despite other lines of evidence.

4) Contamination authentication protocols
- Adopt dedicated clean‑room standards appropriate to each biomolecule target: aDNA in ISO Class 7 clean rooms with unidirectional workflow; enamel proteomics in separate, dedicated clean facilities with positive pressure and HEPA filtration; strict tool sterilization and chain‑of‑custody logs. 
- Negative extraction controls: minimum one per 6–8 samples; reagent blanks in every batch; require replication across independent extractions and runs; enzyme activity controls for proteomics and damage patterns for aDNA authentication across at least two independent labs.

5) Dating cross‑validation plan
- Require at least two independent laboratories per dating method (OSL, ESR/U‑series, cosmogenic) with blind sample IDs; implement a formal discordance threshold (e.g., >2σ or >10% relative difference) triggering reanalysis or method reweighting; include a third‑lab arbitration unless resolution is achieved.

6) preregistration, reproducibility, and data archiving
- Pre‑register hypotheses, priors, and model structures on OSF; manage code in Git with Docker/Singularity containers; archive raw chronometric and molecular data in Zenodo/PANGAEA with versioned releases; ensure metadata align with Dublin Core, ISO19115, and Darwin Core standards.

7) Contingency budgeting and escalation
- Establish predefined triggers for budget reallocation (e.g., biomolecular yield <10% after two seasons), sampling intensification thresholds, and alternate publication paths; document decision rules for postponing analyses or shifting focus to higher‑yield sites.

These concrete deliverables will operationalize Meeting 2 guidance, ensure cross‑lab robustness, and integrate geoarchaeological, chronological, and molecular streams into a coherent, testable framework.
