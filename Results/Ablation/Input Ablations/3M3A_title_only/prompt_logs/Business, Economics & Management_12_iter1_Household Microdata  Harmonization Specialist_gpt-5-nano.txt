Research questions
1. How much of recent changes in household income inequality in CEE countries is driven by demographic aging (changes in age-structure) versus changes in within-age-group inequality?
2. How do these contributions differ by household type (single older adults, older couples, working-age families with/without children, multigenerational households)?
3. What roles do pension systems, labor market attachment, and intergenerational transfers play in mediating the aging–inequality relationship?
4. Are observed patterns causal—i.e., do policy shocks to old-age income (pension reforms, retirement-age changes) produce measurable shifts in household income inequality?

Key concepts and outcome
- Outcome: equivalized disposable household income (OECD-modified scale) at household level; primary inequality measures: Gini, Theil (GE(0), GE(1)), top/bottom income shares, and selected percentile gaps.
- Household-type taxonomy: single elderly (65+), elderly couples, prime-age couples with children, single parents, multigenerational households; age defined by reference person and by household age composition.

Data sources
- EU-SILC microdata (cross-country, repeated cross-sections; main source for CEE 2004–present), supplemented with LIS where useful for longer time coverage and harmonization, and SHARE for detailed older-adult income and health. National panels where available for causal/panel estimations.

Decomposition and estimation strategy
1. Two-part decomposition of change in inequality: (a) composition effect (age-structure/household-type shares) and (b) within-group inequality effect. Use Das Gupta/Kitagawa style accounting to decompose changes in mean incomes and inequality indices.
