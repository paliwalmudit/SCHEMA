- Withdrawal for pseudonymized data: trigger mapping-key destruction, purge pseudonymized derivatives, notify data recipients, maintain audit trail; if data is irreversibly anonymized, provide participant-acknowledgement of limits on withdrawal.
- Special protections: enhanced consent options, opt-outs for vulnerable groups, and robust redress channels.

5) Legal pathway and governance
- Stakeholders: ministries (MEXT, PPC), METI, JSPS, university consortia, RECs, civil society.
- Timeline: PPC/guidance 18–24 months; statutory amendments 24–36 months.
- Model language: institutional REC approval for secondary use with proportionate safeguards, explicit withdrawal mechanisms, and DPO oversight.
- Accreditation and audit cadence: annual REC and DPO audits; public incident reporting; sanctions via funder actions.

6) Reproducible evaluation design
- Sampling: nationwide surveys (n≈1,500–2,000); 40–60 interviews; 20 institutions in pilot.
- Outcomes: time-to-decision, template uptake, stakeholder satisfaction, privacy incidents.
- Analysis plan: preanalysis protocol, multiple-imputation for missing data, publicly archived scripts.

7) Budget and timeline
- Three-year plan: staffing ¥90M, registry/IT ¥60M, secure analysis environments ¥30M, training ¥20M, audits/evaluation ¥25M, contingency ¥25M.
- Phases: design (0–6mo); build/pilot (6–18mo); evaluation (18–30mo); scale/policy alignment (30–36mo).

8) Tooling, training, and monitoring
- Registry API/UI: public metadata with restricted detailed views; immutable audit logs.
- Safe analysis environment: RBAC, encryption, secure data access.
- Training: REC and researcher curriculum; DPO workflows; multilingual materials.
- KPIs: time-to-decision, uptake rate, privacy incidents per 1,000 projects, stakeholder-satisfaction DLPs. Decision rules: progress thresholds trigger scale-up or redesign.

These deliverables yield concrete, reproducible pilot specifications, balancing protection, practicality, and policy alignment.
