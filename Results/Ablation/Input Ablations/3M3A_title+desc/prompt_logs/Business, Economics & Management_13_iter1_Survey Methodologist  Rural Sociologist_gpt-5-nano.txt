- Response, nonresponse, and data quality
  - Monitor response propensity by region, farm size, and prior participation; implement tailored follow‑ups (phone/SMS/paper); set a realistic response target and report nonresponse analyses.
  - Use early vs late respondent comparisons and auxiliary data to assess and adjust for potential biases; document limitations transparently.

- Analytical framework and modeling
  - Use multilevel models with farms nested in regions; consider cross‑classified models when farms participate in multiple innovations; implement random slopes for innovation effects.
  - Employ propensity‑type methods or inverse probability weighting to support causal inference between adopters and non‑adopters; plan difference‑in‑differences with longitudinal data when available.
  - Develop segmentation analyses (cluster or latent class) to identify adopter types and tailor trial designs.

- Data governance and preregistration
  - Pre‑register primary and secondary outcomes, estimands, and multiplicity control; publish code and analysis notebooks with version control; adopt standardized metadata and data dictionaries.
  - Establish consent, anonymization, and controlled data sharing; provide participant‑centered feedback reports and aggregated dashboards.

- Extension and trial linkage
  - Align survey sampling with 2–3 pilot regions for on‑farm trials; map identified innovations to regional sites; plan annual synthesis workshops and extension materials to facilitate rapid learning and scaling.

- Timeline and deliverables
  - Deliver finalized sampling protocol, survey instrument, and pre‑registered analysis plan within the next quarter; initiate baseline sampling concurrent with SOP training; prepare open‑data and code repositories for transparency.

This integrated plan strengthens representativeness, enhances measurement validity, ensures rigorous linkage between survey insights and participatory trials, and supports transparent, scalable extension decisions.
