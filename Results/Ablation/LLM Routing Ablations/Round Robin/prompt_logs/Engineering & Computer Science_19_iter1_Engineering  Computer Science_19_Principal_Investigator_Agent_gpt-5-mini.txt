Theorem (OOB split‑conformal approximate marginal validity, informal). Let (Z1,...,Zn) be exchangeable training examples and let S_i be per‑sample OOB conformity scores computed by aggregating only trees that omitted sample i. Suppose the family {S_i} is identically distributed with pairwise correlation bounded by ρ ≥ 0. Let n_cal be the number of calibration samples and let Q̂ be the empirical (1−α)-quantile of the S_i. Then the split‑conformal prediction sets built using Q̂ satisfy
Coverage ≥ 1−α − δ(n_cal,ρ),
where δ(n_cal,ρ) = O( sqrt(ρ / n_cal) ). More precisely, by Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz applied with an “effective sample size” n_eff = n_cal/(1+(n_cal−1)ρ),
P(coverage < 1−α − ε) ≤ 2 exp(−2 n_eff ε^2).

Proof sketch. Dependence inflates variance of the empirical CDF; pairwise correlation upper bound yields the n_eff expression via variance decomposition. Apply DKW (or Hoeffding) replacing n_cal by n_eff to bound quantile estimation error, which maps to coverage shortfall.

Interpretation. If trees share many common structures so ρ is not negligible, coverage degrades at rate ~sqrt(ρ/n_cal). When ρ→0 (independent OOB scores) we recover usual finite‑sample DKW bounds.

2) Randomized thinning quantitative guidance
Mechanism. If each sample’s OOB score is computed from a random subset of trees of fraction t (independently per sample, with replacement or without), pairwise correlation scales roughly as ρ_t ≈ t^2 ρ0 where ρ0 is the correlation at t=1. Therefore δ(n_cal,ρ_t) ≈ O( sqrt(t^2 ρ0 / n_cal) ) = t · O( sqrt(ρ0 / n_cal) ). Practical rule: to halve dependence-driven error, thin trees by ~1/2; choose t by solving t ≤ (target δ) · sqrt(n_cal/ρ0). Validate ρ0 by small pilot runs.
