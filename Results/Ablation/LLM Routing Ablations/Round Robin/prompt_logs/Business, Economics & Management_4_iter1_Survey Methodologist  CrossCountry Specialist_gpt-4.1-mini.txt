Fourth, during fieldwork, implement rigorous real-time quality control including minimum response-duration thresholds, consistency checks, and attention probes to detect speeders and satisficers. The pre-registered exclusion criteria will combine these quality metrics with comprehension checks to maintain internal validity. Fifth, a post-stratification weighting scheme will be developed and pre-registered to adjust residual inter-country differences in demographics and environmental-attitudinal profiles after quota implementation, enhancing representativeness and comparability of samples across the Netherlands and Denmark.

Sixth, collaboration with the Lifecycle & Externalities Valuation Specialist is critical to ensure that country-specific monetized external costs are correctly and consistently integrated into the survey translation, label text, and numeric formatting. The use of local currency with locale-appropriate numeric separators and formatting conventions will minimize measurement error and respondent confusion. This alignment includes embedding credible source notes and the third-party seal orthogonally, as per the factorial design.

Finally, full documentation of the sampling frame, recruitment processes, quality control, exclusion decisions, weighting methodology, and equivalence diagnostics will be included in the pre-analysis plan and final study reports to ensure transparency and replicability. Publishing detailed metadata on response rates, attrition patterns, and quality metrics will facilitate independent verification and policy uptake.

This rigorous, culturally sensitive, and methodologically harmonized cross-country survey implementation strategy will maximize data integrity, enable robust causal inferences, and support nuanced heterogeneity and equity analyses. These strengths position the study to produce actionable insights for policymakers and retailers on designing and deploying true-price labels that effectively guide sustainable consumer choices in both the Dutch and Danish markets.
