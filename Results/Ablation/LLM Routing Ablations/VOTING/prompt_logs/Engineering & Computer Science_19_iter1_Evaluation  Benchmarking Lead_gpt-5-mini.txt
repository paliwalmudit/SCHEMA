- Document and fix deterministic tie-breaking behavior (randomized tie-breaking or mid-p adjustment) with random_state for reproducibility; ties affect exact finite-sample validity for discrete conformity scores (classification).

Method-selection guidance (practical + theoretical)
- Split conformal: simplest and fastest; provably valid under exchangeability; less efficient (wider sets) because only a calibration fold is used.
- CV+: good compromise when cross-validation is affordable; reuses data for prediction and calibration and tends to be tighter than split conformal. Use when training cost is moderate and you want improved efficiency.
- Jackknife+-after-bootstrap: preferred when using bootstrap ensembles (standard RF) and when you can implement correct exclusion via inbag masks. It reuses models and often gives competitive/tighter sets without full K-fold refits; it has finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability when the OOB exclusion is exact. Use this as default for large-scale random-forest workflows where your plan speeds up OOB aggregation.
- Use CV+ if model instability or small n suggests jackknife-style approximations may be conservative or less stable.

Adaptive prediction sets (classification)
- Use nonconformity score 1 - p_y (or score based on ranked cumulative probabilities) and calibrate the threshold on OOB predictions. For discrete ties apply randomized tie-breaking or mid-p to recover exact-level guarantees.
- For multiclass store per-leaf class-counts and compute p_y in the kernel; ensure normalization and consistent smoothing (if using Laplace smoothing, document its effect on validity).

- Edge-case tests: heavy class imbalance, repeated covariates, features causing many identical leaf assignments, and varying inbag counts in bootstrap.

Practical caveats
- Exchangeability is required for guarantees; non-IID data (time series, covariate shift) breaks exact coverage—document and provide warnings.
