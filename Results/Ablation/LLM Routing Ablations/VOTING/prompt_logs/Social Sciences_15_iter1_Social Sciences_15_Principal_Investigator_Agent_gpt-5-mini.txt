  "Abstract": "This study tests whether texts generated by ChatGPT-4 from fourth-grade students' own prompts improve reading skills, comprehension, and reading motivation. We use a sequential explanatory mixed-method design combining a Solomon four-group experiment and a purposive case-study strand in two public primary schools. The intervention produces short, curriculum-aligned texts from each student prompt, subjects them to automated safety and readability checks, and routes them through a teacher accept/reject workflow before classroom use. Primary quantitative analysis estimates the intent-to-treat effect on a composite reading comprehension score using repeated measures and mixed models; secondary analyses include per-protocol and CACE (complier-average causal effect) estimators. Qualitative case studies explain mechanisms through observations, interviews, and artifact analysis. The main contribution is a reproducible, ethically framed protocol that evaluates personalized AI-generated texts for elementary reading outcomes and documents implementation, safety, and fidelity factors that shape effectiveness. Expected impact is clearer evidence on whether student-driven AI text generation is a viable classroom tool for improving reading outcomes.",
  "Methodology": "Participants and design: Recruit fourth-grade students in two public schools (target N=106 for the pilot/full study). Use a Solomon four-group design: two cells with a baseline pretest and two without, crossed with treatment (ChatGPT texts) and control (standard classroom texts). Randomize at the student level when feasible; stratify randomization by school, baseline reading tercile, and ELL status. Intervention workflow: each student creates short prompts; prompts undergo automated PII redaction (NER+regex) and are then used with a frozen ChatGPT-4 system prompt to generate two text variants. Automated checks compute readability (Flesch–Kincaid), topical alignment (embedding cosine similarity), vocabulary counts, and a safety classifier. Texts failing thresholds are auto-regenerated up to two times, then routed to teacher/RA review. Teachers review and accept/reject or make minor edits through a compact UI; all decisions and edit magnitudes are logged. Curriculum scaffolds: each text includes up to three new word definitions, one or two comprehension questions, and tiered scaffolding prompts. Measurement: administer a composite reading comprehension battery (multiple-choice, cloze, short constructed response) and validated reading-motivation scales. Collect repeated measures: pretest (when assigned) and three posttests (immediate, 3 weeks, 8–10 weeks). Analysis plan: primary estimand is ITT average treatment effect estimated via linear mixed models (random intercepts for student and class, AR(1) residuals) on repeated measures; report the Solomon unbiased contrast and test pretest sensitization. Secondary estimands: per-protocol (teacher accept-rate ≥80%) and CACE using assignment as an instrument. Qualitative strand: purposive sample of 10–14 students and 4–6 teachers for observations, interviews, and artifact analysis; use thematic coding and joint displays to link mechanisms to quantitative variation.",
  "Experiments": "Participants and datasets: Pilot N=30–40 stratified by baseline ability to calibrate instruments and thresholds; full study target N=106 across two schools.   "Risk Factors and Limitations": "Underpowered effects: with N=106 and classroom clustering small true effects (d≈0.30) may not be detectable. Contamination: students may share generated texts across arms, biasing estimates. Safety and PII: automated filters can fail, risking exposure of harmful content or identifiable data. Model drift and outages: provider updates can change outputs; outages require a vetted fallback corpus. Teacher burden: review time may exceed targets, affecting fidelity and feasibility. Measurement bias: instruments may not be fully invariant across ELL or special-needs students, limiting comparability. Generalizability: results may not extend beyond fourth graders, the two participating schools, or different curricular contexts. Reliance on vendor API: data residency, legal, and operational constraints limit reproducibility if access changes. Technical failures: embedding or readability thresholds may misclassify topicality leading to unnecessary human reviews. Ethical constraints: IRB or district restrictions could limit data retention, analysis, or sharing of generation logs."
