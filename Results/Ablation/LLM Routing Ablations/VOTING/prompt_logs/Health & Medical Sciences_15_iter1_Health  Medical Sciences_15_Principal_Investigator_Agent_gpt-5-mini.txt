  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "1) Underreporting and identifiability: capture–recapture relies on overlap and assumptions; sparse overlaps or violations (dependence, heterogeneity) can bias estimates and make some strata non-identifiable. 2) Matching error: hashed identifiers and limited linking fields can produce false matches or missed matches; manual review sample may be insufficient to fully quantify error for all subgroups. 3) Small counts: sigma and DPMO are unstable for rare events; many low-volume tests may not meet reporting thresholds, limiting per-test conclusions. 4) Temporal confounding: concurrent system changes (instrument/reagent switches, staffing shifts, pandemic waves) may bias ITS estimates and causal claims. 5) Missingness: key covariates may be MNAR; imputation relies on MAR assumptions and MNAR sensitivity ranges may not capture true bias. 6) NLP limits: free-text narratives may be short, inconsistent, or in mixed languages, leading to class imbalance and poor model performance for rare categories. 7) Data governance delays: IRB/DUA or EHR access delays can limit linkage and outcome attribution. 8) Generalizability: findings from one hospital and its local test mix and workflows may not generalize to other settings. 9) Resource limits: large sample sizes required to detect small relative improvements may exceed feasible data or study periods."
