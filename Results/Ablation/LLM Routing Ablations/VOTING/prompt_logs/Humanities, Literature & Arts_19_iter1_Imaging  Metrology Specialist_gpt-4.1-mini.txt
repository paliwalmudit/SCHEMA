Recommended method and tip
- Use instrumented (continuous load–displacement) indentation. This yields load–displacement curves required to obtain hardness and contact stiffness in a single test.
- Prefer a spherical indenter (radius 50–200 µm) for coarse-grained, heterogeneous stones because it averages microstructure and reduces cracking; use a Berkovich (three-sided pyramid) if grain sizes are fine (<~10 µm) and high spatial resolution is needed.

Calibration and machine set-up
- Calibrate tip area function and machine compliance using fused silica and a standard reference glass. Measure thermal drift and null it or correct in data.
- Verify spherical radius or Berkovich area function before tests and re-check after test batches.

Sample preparation and indentation spacing
- Produce a flat area: gently polish to 1–3 µm diamond finish if acceptable; if preserving cortex is required, prepare separate small chips for testing. Flatness and low roughness are crucial: target RMS roughness << intended maximum indentation depth (depth >10× roughness).

Testing parameters and data collection
- Use monotonic loading with a hold at Pmax (10–30 s) to reduce creep effects; record high-sampling-rate load–displacement data.
