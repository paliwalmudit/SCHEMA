Use these targets to decide when to automate vs require human-in-the-loop.

6) Targeted search strategies for rare labels: combine seed keyword lists (including regional-language variants), metadata filters (courses titled “ethics”, “responsible AI”, “data protection”), instructor/department heuristics, and embedding-based retrieval (SBERT/LaBSE) to surface candidates. Prioritize manual review quotas and set a trigger: if positives <30 after first automated pass, execute targeted search and add up to 100 manual-review slots before increasing total n.

7) Active learning and thresholds: tune confidence thresholds per language/category on held-out validation data rather than using a universal 0.75. Start at 0.75 as the human-review threshold, then adjust to meet minimum-positive and IRR targets. Stop rules: stop active sampling for a category when minimum positives are reached and metric CIs are stable across two successive retraining rounds.

8) IRR reporting and CIs: report Cohen’s kappa and Krippendorff’s alpha, plus prevalence-adjusted kappa, and produce bootstrap (BCa) confidence intervals for all IRR measures when per-language samples are small.

9) Annotator staffing and time budgeting: estimate excerpt annotation at ~10–20 minutes on average; double-annotating 240 docs (≈480 annotations) implies ~80–160 annotator-hours; budget adjudication at 25–30% extra and reserve 20–30% buffer for oversampling and retraining. Maintain a 1:4 adjudicator-to-annotator ratio and continuous calibration sessions.

These specifications make the pilot scientifically defensible, provide clear escalation triggers for rare categories, and align annotation practice with the project’s IRR, classifier, and policy goals.
