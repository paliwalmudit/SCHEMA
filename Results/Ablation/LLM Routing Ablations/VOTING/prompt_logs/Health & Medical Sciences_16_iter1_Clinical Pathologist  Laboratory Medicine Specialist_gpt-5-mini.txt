1) AG calculation and reporting
- Compute and report both AG_including_K = (Na+ + K+) − (Cl− + HCO3−) and AG_excluding_K = Na+ − (Cl− + HCO3−), and explicitly state which was used in each analysis.  
- Always present albumin-corrected AG using: AG_corr = measured AG + 2.5 × (4.0 − albumin [g/dL]), and run analyses with and without this correction because albumin is a dominant determinant of AG.

2) Lactate assay and specimen considerations
- Capture assay type (central lab enzymatic vs point-of-care blood gas analyzer), sample type (venous, arterial, capillary), tube type and handling (fluoride/oxalate vs plain), and time-to-analysis. Pre-analytic glycolysis (delayed processing, no antiglycolytic tube, prolonged tourniquet) can artifactually raise lactate. Flag POC vs central lab because systematic biases of ~0.2–0.5 mmol/L and differing imprecision are common. Analyze arterial and venous lactates separately or adjust for type in multivariable models.

3) Analytical variability and documentation
- Record analyzer make/model, assay method (lactate oxidase or LDH-based), calibration schedule, and internal QC (within-run and between-run CV) and external quality results if available. Typical analytical CVs for lactate are instrument-dependent but often 2–6% at clinically relevant concentrations; small systematic biases in Na, Cl, or HCO3 (1–3 mmol/L) will materially shift AG and should be considered. If multi-site or mixed analyzers are used, include assay/site as covariates or perform sensitivity analyses.

4) Biologic and clinical confounders to consider
- Hypoalbuminemia lowers measured AG independent of unmeasured acids; hence the albumin correction. Renal failure, hyperphosphatemia, ketoacidosis, toxic alcohols, and advanced liver disease add unmeasured ions that dissociate AG-lactate correlation; consider excluding or performing stratified analyses. Note that lactate is only one contributor to high AG; a high AG does not uniquely imply elevated lactate.

- Report ROC/AUC for both measured and albumin-corrected AG and for AG variants with/without K.
