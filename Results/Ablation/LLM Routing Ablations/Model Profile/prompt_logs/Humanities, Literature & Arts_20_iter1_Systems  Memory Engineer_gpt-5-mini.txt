Key operational definitions

- Object: the external referent (environmental state, event, agent belief) that signs purport to denote. Objects include indexical features (time, location, causal events).
- Interpretant: the system’s internal disposition or representational update that mediates further sign use (i.e., the change in internal state that informs subsequent outputs and actions).
- Indexical grounding: reliable, intervention-sensitive covariance between internal representations and external objects across contexts and agents.

Measurable constructs and metrics
- Indexicality score: reproducible covariance between a model’s internal representation and measured external variables under controlled interventions (e.g., mutual information across do-interventions).
- Referential stability: the probability the model correctly re-identifies the same object after transformations (occlusion, re-description, temporal delay).
- Abductive inference rate: frequency and quality of novel, explanatory hypotheses generated from sparse evidence, measured via human evaluation and predictive utility.
- Social repair success: rate at which the model updates interpretants in response to human correction and uses those updates in future interactions.
- Causal alignment metric: improvement in prediction under interventional tests (do-calculus style) versus observational-only baselines.

Testable hypotheses
1. Models with persistent, timestamped memory plus sensorimotor or mediated environmental feedback will show higher indexicality scores than stateless LLMs trained only on static text.
2. A model’s ability to function as an interpretant correlates with its abduction rate and with successful social repair—i.e., models that generate useful explanatory hypotheses also better assimilate corrective signals.
3. Embedding models within social-epistemic routines (triangulated human communities, corrective feedback loops) produces interpretants more aligned with human community interpretants than solitary training regimes.

- Controlled referent-intervention suite: tasks where referents are manipulated (moved, renamed, masked) while measuring internal representations and outputs to compute indexicality and referential stability.
