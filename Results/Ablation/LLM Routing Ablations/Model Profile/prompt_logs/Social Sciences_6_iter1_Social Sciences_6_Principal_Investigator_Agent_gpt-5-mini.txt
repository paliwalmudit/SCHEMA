We combine a two-phase qualitative expert engagement (exploratory interviews then focus groups) with a reproducible CLD (causal loop diagram) weighting procedure and sensitivity testing, and link coded qualitative claims to routine quantitative data (incident reports, claims, and sensor streams) using time-windowed matching. The main contribution is an operational, testable protocol that produces provenance-tagged CLDs, quantitatively validated causal links, and pilot-ready indicators for multi-site replication. Expected outcomes are (1) empirical CLDs with uncertainty quantification, (2) mapped indicators that track feedback loop functioning, and (3) evidence that circular-ecosystem designs yield larger incident-rate reductions than linear designs, informing scalable OSH policy and practice.",
  "Methodology": "1) Qualitative phase: conduct 24–30 semi-structured expert interviews across 4–6 countries, coded with a shared codebook. Code each excerpt with NodeID, polarity (+, -, ±, 0), coder strength (1–3), timestamp, and exemplar quote. 2) CLD assembly: aggregate coded links into a causal loop diagram. Compute a composite link weight using a defined formula that combines frequency, median coder strength, and cross-source corroboration. 3) Sensitivity testing: run bootstraps (resample transcripts) and Monte-Carlo draws on coder strength to produce uncertainty intervals for each link weight and flag unstable links. (Bootstrap = repeated sampling with replacement; Monte-Carlo = random sampling from assumed distributions.) 4) Tagging and linkage: attach NodeID and timestamp to excerpts and match to routine data by SiteID within a ±14-day window to create event timelines. 5) Quantitative validation: treat validated CLD links as directional hypotheses and test them with interrupted time-series (ITS) or lagged correlation models using site-level time series. ITS uses segmented regression with AR(1) correction. 6) Pilot deployment: run multiple-case replication across 4 sites, collect sensor streams, incident reports, and engagement logs. 7) Governance and ethics: perform DPIA per site, apply GDPR principles as baseline, and document consent and retention rules per jurisdiction. 8) Documentation: keep provenance metadata for each CLD link indicating actor/source and evidence.",
  "Experiments": "Datasets: (A) Qualitative transcripts from 24–30 interviews (4–6 countries); (B) Routine site data per site for 24 months total (12 months pre-intervention + 12 months post-intervention) including time-stamped incident reports, insurer claims, and sensor streams (high-frequency time-series); (C) Pilot monitoring logs (attendance, MOUs, actions). Baselines: (1) standard linear intervention sites (control) that implement comparable safety measures without explicit circular-ecosystem design; (2) CLD assembled with unweighted or rank-based aggregation as method baseline; (3) CLD with composite weight but with cross-source corroboration C set to zero (ablation). Evaluation metrics: primary = incident rate change (incidents per 100 FTE) measured monthly; secondary = cost per averted incident, Actor Engagement Index (% targeted actors participating), data completeness (% required fields), median reporting latency (hours), fidelity score (0–100), AIM acceptability mean. Statistical tests and setup: 1) For each site, run segmented regression ITS with AR(1) error to estimate level and slope changes post-intervention; significance alpha=0.05, cluster-robust SEs. 2) Multi-site inference via mixed-effects segmented regression with random intercepts for sites. 3) CLD validation: for each hypothesized link, compute directional concordance between qualitative-predicted change and quantitative indicator using sign test and Kendall's tau for ranked agreement. 4) Ablation studies: compare predictive validity (cross-validated R2 and AIC) of composite-weight CLD vs rank-based CLD and composite CLD without C. 5) Power/simulation: assume baseline monthly incident rate = 0.42 per 100 FTE (≈5/year), overdispersion factor = 1.5, target minimum detectable relative reduction = 10% with 80% power; run Monte-Carlo simulations per site to confirm sample adequacy.   "Risk Factors and Limitations": "1) Data completeness: sensor failures, connectivity outages, or under-reporting of incidents may create large missing segments in time-series. 2) Small-sample inference: limited number of sites or short pre/post windows will reduce ITS power and limit causal claims. 3) Confounding and identification: lack of randomization and concurrent external changes can bias ITS estimates; omitted time-varying confounders remain a threat. 4) Coder variability: inter-coder disagreement on polarity or strength can produce unstable CLD links and reduce reproducibility. 5) Cross-national legal constraints: data transfer restrictions and differing retention rules can limit aggregation and comparability. 6) Sensor data biases: measurement drift, calibration errors, and heterogeneous device performance across sites can distort quantitative validation. 7) Generalization limits: findings may not generalize beyond sampled sectors, countries, or organizational sizes. 8) Compute and time limits: extensive bootstraps and Monte-Carlo runs on large transcript corpora and high-frequency streams may require substantial compute time and storage."
