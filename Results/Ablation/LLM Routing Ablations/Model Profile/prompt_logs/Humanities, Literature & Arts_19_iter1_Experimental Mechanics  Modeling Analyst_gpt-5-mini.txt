5) Uncertainty propagation. Propagate indent posterior uncertainty and emulator uncertainty by drawing emulator posterior predictive samples and multiplying sampled E_op/H_op draws by f_E/f_H draws to produce corrected E/H posterior samples; report medians and 95% credible intervals and archive full posterior draws.

6) Fracture‑aware modelling and statistics. Detect pop‑ins/cracks by L–D diagnostics (e.g., displacement jumps or load drops >5% peak) and route flagged indents to a fracture branch. Simulate fracture (cohesive elements) on a representative subset to quantify bias. Implement a Bayesian mixture model with elastic and fracture regimes and/or censored likelihoods for partially failed indents, and calibrate P(fracture | d/a, porosity, anisotropy).

7) Validation thresholds and workflow gating. Require synthetic recovery tests (simulated indents + noise) with bias <5% and RMSE ≤10% for corrected E/H. Empirically validate against RUS or 4‑point bending on matched samples using Bland‑Altman plots; require RMSE ≤10% and no systematic bias across petrographic classes. Apply correction maps only inside emulator training bounds; out‑of‑bounds cases require bespoke simulation.

8) Practical software and reproducibility. Use open (FEniCS, CalculiX) or commercial (ABAQUS/ANSYS) solvers, store meshes, seeds, solver settings, unit tests and synthetic test suites in Git with CI, and deliver Docker/Singularity containers. Expect ~500–2,000 CPU‑hours per lithology for ensembles; document and archive all inputs and outputs on GitHub + Zenodo.

These operational rules provide a clear path to robust, uncertainty‑aware mapping from single indents to bulk E and H for the Sibhudu study.
