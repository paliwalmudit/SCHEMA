To empirically validate the exclusion restriction, we must conduct placebo regressions predicting pre-appointment earnings management levels and unrelated outcomes (e.g., CAPEX growth), along with balance tests assessing correlation of instruments with pretreatment firm characteristics and regional economic or political factors. Including detailed district-level controls such as GDP per capita, industry composition, urbanization measures, and province election fixed effects will further reduce confounding, and falsification tests on financial-sector firms outside the main sample will add robustness.

Regarding political-connection controls, I advocate integrating firm-level indicators derived from multiple data sources: state ownership or SOE affiliation from SECP filings; government contracts and procurement expenditures documented in public procurement databases and annual reports; ownership or board ties to political officeholders and election donors identified via linking SECP records with election-donor registries; plus temporal and spatial political variables capturing election years and province-level political volatility. These controls should be fully pre-specified and harmonized to mitigate omitted-variable bias stemming from political-military entanglement.

To address audit-fee endogeneity concerns given Pakistan’s data constraints, the chosen approach will treat audit fees as one proxy within a broader audit-quality composite rather than as a standalone explanatory variable. We will implement propensity score matching to balance observable characteristics across firms with varying audit fees and auditor types, supplemented by difference-in-differences analyses exploiting auditor switches to isolate exogenous variation in fee structures. Additionally, a Heckman selection model will be applied if sample patterns suggest selective fee disclosure, with first-stage diagnostics and identification tests carefully pre-registered. Where feasible, exploratory IV candidates for fees—such as local Big Four office presence or geographic distance to auditors—will be tested but treated as auxiliary.

For the audit-quality composite, I propose constructing the primary index via principal component analysis (PCA) combining Big Four auditor affiliation, standardized auditor tenure, normalized audit-fee residuals, modified audit opinion indicators, and a textual analysis-derived auditor-report concern score. This PCA-based index should be validated by its correlations with external governance proxies like restatement likelihood and accrual conservatism. Robustness variants including additive indices with equal weights and single-proxy regressions will be pre-registered.

Regarding Roychowdhury’s REM measures, pre-registered robustness checks should include alternative scaling by assets and sales, disaggregation of REM into abnormal CFO, production costs, and discretionary expenses, and correlation with operational metrics such as inventory turnover and days sales outstanding to address data quality sensitivity. We will also conduct power analyses specifying minimum detectable effect sizes (~0.10–0.15 standardized betas) to ensure sufficient sample size coverage.

For inference, I recommend firm-level clustering of standard errors as primary, complemented by two-way clustering by firm and industry-year to control for cross-sectional dependence, plus wild-cluster bootstrap methods in smaller cluster-count contexts. Multiple hypothesis testing corrections (Benjamini–Hochberg) will be applied to limit false discovery rates.

Finally, pre-registration documents must clearly delineate primary versus secondary hypotheses, detail exact regression equations including variable transformations such as 1%/99% winsorization thresholds, sample inclusion and exclusion criteria, and specify all fixed effects and clustering rules upfront. Sub-sample heterogeneity analyses by ownership types, board structure, CEO duality, and firm size will be outlined to further address omitted-variable concerns and contextual interpretation boundaries.

This integrated identification, control, and robustness plan responds directly to prior high-priority feedback, advancing credible causal claims on how military experience and audit quality jointly govern earnings management in Pakistan’s complex emerging-market context. It equips the research team with transparent, testable, and replicable empirical tools to deliver robust and policy-relevant findings.
