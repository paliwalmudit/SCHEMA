Methodologically, we derive a latent satisfaction score via confirmatory factor analysis or item-response modeling, apply inverse-probability weighting for survey response, and compare two attrition corrections: inverse-probability-of-censoring weights (IPCW) and a shared-parameter joint longitudinal–survival model that links mixed-effects revenue to a hazard for churn. Primary estimation uses bias-corrected customer fixed-effects dynamic panels with restricted cubic splines for nonlinearity; IV (2SLS FE) is used only conditional on strict validation. The main contributions are sustained, individual-level causal estimates of satisfaction on revenue, characterization of diminishing returns across tiers, and a prespecified sensitivity and reproducibility suite to support credible inference.",
  "Methodology": "1) Data linkage and panels: link survey records to bank transactions inside a secure enclave; build customer-year panels with baseline window (−12 months) and outcome windows Year1 = months 1–12, Year2 = 13–24, Year3 = 25–36, Year4 = 37–48 after survey completion date t0. 2) Latent satisfaction: fit CFA or IRT to survey items to produce a latent score (one-dimensional). Technical note: CFA/IRT estimate a latent variable that explains item responses; reliability (omega) measures score precision. 3) Survey nonresponse: estimate response propensity with logistic regression and gradient-boosted trees; compute inverse-probability weights (IPW), trim at the 99th percentile and cap max weight at 10. 4) Attrition/churn: implement IPCW using a discrete-time hazard model for staying; in parallel fit a shared-parameter joint model that links a linear mixed model for annual revenue (customer random intercept/slope) to a Cox or discrete hazard for time-to-churn via shared random effects. Shared random effects mean unobserved customer tendencies affect both revenue and churn. 5) Primary estimation: bias-corrected fixed-effects dynamic panel (Kiviet/Bruno style) with customer and calendar-year fixed effects; secondary: system-GMM. Include restricted cubic splines (3 knots) on latent satisfaction to capture nonlinearity; also report tiered estimates (detractor/neutral/promoter). 6) IVs: pre-specify instruments (randomized invite timing/mode, branch operational shocks, scheduled IT outages) and run strict validation (first-stage F>10, partial R2, placebo outcomes, covariate balance, McCrary/event-time checks). 7) Measurement error: if reliability ≥0.70 apply regression calibration (divide naive coefficient by reliability); also run Bayesian latent-variable models to propagate uncertainty. 8) Joint accounts: primary allocation = proportional by average account balance; produce sensitivity variants (exclude joint accounts; equal split). 9) Inference: cluster standard errors by customer and by branch where appropriate; use wild-cluster bootstrap if cluster counts are small. 10) Reproducibility: provide Dockerized code, synthpop de-identified data, and full analysis scripts.",

  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "1) Residual confounding: unobserved time-varying factors correlated with satisfaction and future revenue may bias estimates. 2) IV invalidity: instruments (invite timing/mode, branch shocks) may violate exclusion if they affect revenue via channels other than satisfaction. 3) Attrition MNAR: informative censoring beyond modeled covariates can bias IPCW and joint-model results. 4) Measurement error: low reliability of satisfaction items reduces power and can produce noisy corrections; regression-calibration assumes classical measurement error. 5) Joint-account misallocation: erroneously assigning revenue in joint accounts can bias individual growth estimates. 6) Outcome distribution: revenue is skewed with zeros and heavy tails; transformations may not fully resolve heteroskedasticity. 7) Instrument weakness and small cluster counts: weak first-stage or few branches reduce credibility of IV and clustered inference. 8) Generalizability: results from one Swedish retail bank sample may not transfer to different markets or business models. 9) Data governance constraints: privacy rules may limit public sharing and external validation of linked data. 10) Computational scale: joint Bayesian models and large Monte-Carlo simulations can be compute-intensive and slow."
