  "Risk Factors and Limitations": "1) Fracture and pop‑ins: high fracture rates can reduce usable indents and bias E/H; flagged indents require reliable fracture modelling. 2) Representativity: small contacts may fail RVE criteria (a/g <3 or d/g <1), producing biased OP estimates and forcing use of larger tip radii or extrapolation by emulators. 3) Heterogeneity: multimodal grain distributions, seams, or alteration can produce spatially variable E/H that single indents cannot fully sample. 4) Emulator extrapolation: correction emulators are unreliable outside training bounds; out‑of‑bounds cases need bespoke FEM runs. 5) Computational cost: FEM ensembles and emulator training require hundreds to thousands of CPU‑hours; limited compute can restrict parameter sweeps. 6) Calibration and instrument differences: variation across machines (tip wear, compliance) affects comparability; cross‑lab harmonization is required. 7) Sample access and ethics: museum or site restrictions may limit destructive or surface testing, reducing sample representativity. 8) Archaeological confounding: raw‑material selection and tool shape are influenced by availability, reduction stage, and knapper behavior; mechanical predictors may be correlated but not causal. 9) Statistical power: insufficient specimens or high within‑specimen variance reduces power to detect selection effects. 10) Generalization: protocols tuned on Sibhudu lithologies may not generalize to other sites or rock types without re‑training emulators and re‑calibrating fracture detectors."
