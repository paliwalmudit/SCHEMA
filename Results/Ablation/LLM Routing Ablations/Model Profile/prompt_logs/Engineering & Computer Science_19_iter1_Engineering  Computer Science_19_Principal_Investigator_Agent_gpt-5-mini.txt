Proof sketch: exchangeability implies that ranks of scores are uniform; apply rank-based quantile argument.

2) Lemma (Tree-partition CV+ with Neff correction). Partition T trees into K disjoint groups and form per-group OOB predictions; let Neff denote the effective number of independent trees for aggregated OOB scores and v := T/Neff be the variance-inflation factor. Under exchangeability across samples and approximate independence across groups, the deviation of the empirical calibration quantile from its nominal value is bounded with probability ≥1−δ by O(√(v log(1/δ)/n)) via the DKW inequality with variance inflation. Proof sketch: treat group-aggregated scores as i.i.d. draws with inflated variance, apply DKW to bound quantile error and inflate the conformal threshold accordingly.

With recorded in-bag indicators across B bootstrap replicates, if for every sample i the number r_i of replicates excluding i satisfies r_i ≥ r_min ≥ 1, then jackknife+-after-bootstrap retains a conservative finite-sample coverage bound; coverage slack worsens as r_min decreases. Practical corollary: require r_min ≥ 5 for reliable small-sample behavior, otherwise use CV+ or apply conservative inflation (see practical corrections).

Neff estimator and CI (reproducible)
Estimate per-tree per-sample scores s_{i,t}. Compute average per-tree correlation ρ̂ across trees (average pairwise Pearson corr of tree score vectors). Define Neff = T / (1 + (T−1)ρ̂). If any r_i is small (r_i < 5) for many samples, coverage can degrade.
