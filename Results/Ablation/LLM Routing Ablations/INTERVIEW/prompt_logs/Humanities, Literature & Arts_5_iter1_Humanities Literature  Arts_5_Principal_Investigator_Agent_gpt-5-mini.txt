  "Abstract": "We introduce an empirical framework to test \"consumption populism\": the claim that business leaders use populist logic to construct \"the people\" and shape consumption. We operationalize a three‑stage process—otherization, homogenization, hegemony—using a multimodal codebook applied to speech, advertising, event, and social media data in Hindi, regional dialects, and English. Our primary method combines validated multilingual affect classifiers and multimodal alignment (text, image, video) with causal inference tools: synthetic control as the primary estimator for regional sales and search interest, complemented by staggered difference‑in‑differences. We preregister temporal windows and mediation tests to evaluate whether affective mobilization (pride/anger) mediates the effect of populist practices on consumption. Contributions include (1) a replicable multimodal indicator set tied to Laclauian theory, (2) validated cross‑language affect models, and (3) a causal analysis linking discursive practices to market outcomes. Expected outcomes are clear, testable evidence on whether and how leader-driven populist framing changes consumer behavior.",
