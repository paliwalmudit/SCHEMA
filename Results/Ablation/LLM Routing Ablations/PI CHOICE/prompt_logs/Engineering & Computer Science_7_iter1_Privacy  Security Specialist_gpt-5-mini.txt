1) Concise mean‑field formalization.
Define p_t = (1/N)∑_i a_i,t and let Δθ_i be the random sampled utility gap for agent i: Δθ_i = θ1_i − θ0_i (sampled from the agent’s arm posteriors). Given a population belief p, each agent chooses charge iff Δθ_i > f(p) (forced charging for deadline exceptions treated separately). The mean‑field map is
Φ(p) = Pr_i(Δθ_i > f(p)),
where the probability is under the current prior/posterior law of Δθ across the population. Fixed points p* satisfy p* = Φ(p*).

2) Existence, uniqueness and contraction condition (practical sufficient condition).
Existence: Φ is continuous on [0,1] so Brouwer guarantees at least one fixed point. Uniqueness and local exponential stability follow if Φ is a contraction near p*: sup_{p∈I}|Φ'(p)| < 1 on an interval I containing p*. A usable sufficient bound is
|Φ'(p)| ≤ L_f · S,
where L_f = sup_p |f'(p)| and S = sup_x pdf_{Δθ}(x) (maximum sensitivity of the exceedance probability to a shift). Thus a practical condition is L_f·S < 1. If this fails, enforce smoothing p ← (1−α)p_new + α p_old with α∈(0,1) so effective Lipschitz becomes α + (1−α)L_f·S < 1.

3) Stability, convergence rates and finite‑N fluctuations.
Linearizing at p* yields local geometric convergence with rate |Φ'(p*)|. Finite‑N deviations are O(1/√N) (CLT/Hoeffding) plus TS exploration noise; hence with high probability p_t remains in an O(1/√N) neighborhood of p* once the transient decays. Thompson sampling exploration introduces persistent stochastic fluctuations whose variance decays as posteriors concentrate (roughly O(1/t) per agent), so transient convergence time scales like O(log(1/ε)/(1−|Φ'(p*)|)) and steady‑state variance scales as O(1/N)+O(exploration).

4) Basins of attraction, oscillations and mitigation.
Compute basins numerically by sweeping initial p0 and sampling Φ iterates with TS noise; detect oscillatory cycles when |Φ'(p*)|>1 or multiple fixed points exist.
