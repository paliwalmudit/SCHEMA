A. Theorem (approximation of scalarized value by preference-conditioned actor)
Assumptions: bounded K-vector rewards ∥r_t∥_∞ ≤ R_max, discount γ∈[0,1), scalarization U(w,v) is L_U-Lipschitz in v (for all w), centralized vector critic Q̂(s,a;φ) approximates true Q*(s,a) coordinate-wise with sup-norm error ε_Q := sup_{s,a,k} |Q̂_k(s,a)−Q^*_k(s,a)|, and the learned policy π_θ(w) induces an action-value mismatch measured by ε_π := sup_{s,w} ∥Q̂(s, (π_θ(w), a_{−·})) − Q̂(s,a^*_w)∥_∞ where a^*_w is the (approx.) best action under Q̂ for preference w. Also assume Lipschitz continuity of Q̂ in actions with constant L_a.
Claim: For any preference w,
|U(w, V^{π_θ}_w) − U(w, V^*_{w})| ≤ L_U · ( 2ε_Q/(1−γ) + ε_π/(1−γ) ) .
Sketch: policy-value error decomposed into (i) critic approximation error accumulated over returns ⇒ ≤ ε_Q/(1−γ) per coordinate, (ii) actor suboptimality w.r.t. approximated critic ⇒ action-value gap bounded by ε_π/(1−γ); scalarization Lipschitz gives multiplicative L_U. Constants scale as O(L_U/(1−γ)). Violation modes: if U non‑Lipschitz, Q̂ discontinuous in w or actor class not sufficiently expressive, bound degrades or vacuous; finite network capacity adds irreducible approximation term ε_approx included in ε_Q/ε_π.

B. Off-policy replay & bias
Mechanism of bias: storing transitions with heterogeneous w causes TD targets to mix different target policies (π_w), so Q-updates use behavioral distribution mismatched to target preference leading to biased Bellman backups. Three schemes:
1) Shared buffer (store w with each transition): inexpensive but biased; acceptable if preference sampling distribution during training matches stored-distribution and critic regularization enforces smoothness in w.
2) Per-preference buffers (discretize w or one buffer per prototype): near-unbiased per-prototype learning, stable, but memory× and poorer sample reuse.
3) Shared buffer + importance weighting (IS): weight TD loss by ρ = p_target(w)/p_buffer(w) to correct preference sampling; unbiased in expectation but high-variance. Diagnostics: periodically compute MSE between on-policy TD errors (rollouts for selected w) and replay TD estimates; if MSE_w grows above threshold τ_MSE (e.g., 2× on-policy MSE), switch to per-pref or add IS.

Scaling drivers: centralized critic input size ∝ M*(dim_obs+dim_act); critic output size ∝ K; replay per transition stores O(M) observations/actions + K rewards.

- M=5,K=5 ≈ ×(≈2.5) input size and ×(≈2.5) output heads.
- M=10,K=10 ≈ ×(≈5) input, ×(≈5) outputs.
- M=20,K=10 ≈ ×(≈10) input, ×(≈5) outputs.
Mitigations with estimated savings:
1) Factorized critic (shared global trunk + per-agent/local factor networks + aggregator) reduces joint-action combinatorial cost to near-linear O(M) vs naive centralized O(expensive coupling); estimated compute/memory savings ~40–70% at M=20.
2) Low-rank / bottlenecked K-heads: represent K-head weight matrix as rank-r (r≪K), saving ~ (1−r/K) of head params; with r=2 and K=10 yields ~80% head-parameter reduction.

D. Marginal-expectation credit assignment
Estimator: A_i(s,a) ≈ Q̂(s,a) − (1/N) Σ_{j=1}^N Q̂(s, (a_i^{(j)}, a_{−i})) where a_i^{(j)}∼π_i(·|o_i,w) or a_i^{(j)} sampled from proposal.
