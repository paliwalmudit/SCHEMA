Occupational Epidemiologist: "Thank you — below I translate the high‑priority feedback into concrete, operational specifications, protocols, sampling examples, validation criteria, leads and timelines to finalize CDM v0.1.

Denominators & conversion algorithms
- Primary denominators: worker‑years (person‑years, PY) and working hours. Report rates per 100,000 PY and per 1,000,000 hours.  
- Conversions: PY = sum(person_days)/365.25; hours = PY * mean_annual_hours_per_worker. If only average weekly hours available: mean_annual_hours = mean_weekly_hours * 52. Use age/sex/occupation strata to compute stratum‑specific mean hours before aggregating. Normalize rates: rate_PY = (cases / PY) * 100,000; rate_hours = (cases / hours) * 1,000,000.

Policy/enforcement/composite index rules
- Component processing: convert components to z‑scores within country-year, then rescale to 0–100 via min‑max for interpretability. Default weighting: equal weights across components. Alternative weight sets: PCA-derived weights and theory-driven weights (pre-specified).  
- Missing components: if ≤20% components missing, impute by multiple imputation chained equations (MICE) using other policy and contextual predictors; otherwise compute available‑case composite with denominator adjusted (sum of weights of observed components) and flag provenance.  
- Sensitivity checks: produce indices under equal weighting, PCA weighting and leave‑one‑out; report rank correlations and plot component impacts.

JEM construction, calibration and validation protocol
- Construction: map ISCO‑08 4‑digit occupations to exposure domains; assign ordinal exposure scores (0–3) per domain with documented rules.  
- Calibration sampling frame: stratified random sampling of occupations by risk stratum and country (target 15–30 jobs per ISCO4 cell where feasible). Aim for minimum 20 personal measurements per high‑risk cell.  
- Gold standard: personal exposure measurements (e.g., noise dosimetry, respirable dust), task observation checklists and repeated measures.  
- Calibration: regression calibration linking JEM score to measured exposure to derive multiplicative/additive calibration factors; quantify uncertainty via bootstrap (1,000 resamples). Propagate calibration uncertainty in downstream RR estimates.

Informal sector workforce denominator methods
- Prefer household labour force surveys and time‑use surveys to estimate workforce counts and hours in informal occupations. Use employment‑to‑population ratios and sector‑specific participation rates.  
- When surveys weak: complement with sentinel surveillance in markets/SMEs, capture‑recapture using administrative lists (tax, health clinics) and survey lists, and small area estimation borrowing strength from national surveys.
