Proposed operational definition (concise and Africa-tailored)
Social cohesion is the multi-dimensional quality of a society that expresses the strength of connectedness, solidarity and mutual obligation among people and between people and public institutions. In African contexts this includes: horizontal trust and cooperative behaviour across identity boundaries (ethnic, religious, migrant/native, rural/urban), vertical trust and perceived legitimacy of formal and customary authorities, felt belonging and inclusion (material and symbolic), patterns of reciprocal exchange and social networks (formal and informal), and the absence of systematic exclusion or structural discrimination that undermines collective life.

Core dimensions and candidate indicators
1. Trust and reciprocity
- Horizontal trust (trust in neighbors/different groups), measures from survey trust items and behavioural games.
- Reciprocity: frequency of mutual aid, remittance networks, collective action participation.

2. Belonging and social identity
- Sense of national/local belonging, willingness to identify with cross-cutting groups, experiences of discrimination/exclusion.

3. Institutional legitimacy and state-society relations
- Vertical trust in government, perceived fairness of service delivery, trust in customary leaders and local authorities.

4. Inclusion and equality
- Perceived and measured access to services, economic inequality indicators, multidimensional marginalization (gender, ethnicity, displacement).

5. Civic engagement and collective action
- Voluntary association membership, protest and petitioning behaviour, participation in local governance and traditional dispute resolution.

6. Social networks and bridging capital
- Network diversity indexes, intergroup ties, migration-linked networks, kin vs non-kin support.

Measurement approach (levels and methods)
- Multi-level design: individual (surveys), community (aggregates, community mapping), regional/national (administrative and macro indicators).
- Mixed methods: household surveys (core module adaptable to Afrobarometer/DHS), social network modules, participatory qualitative mapping, targeted behavioural experiments, and administrative/service-delivery data.
- Longitudinal/panel elements to capture dynamics after shocks (conflict, epidemics, economic shocks).
