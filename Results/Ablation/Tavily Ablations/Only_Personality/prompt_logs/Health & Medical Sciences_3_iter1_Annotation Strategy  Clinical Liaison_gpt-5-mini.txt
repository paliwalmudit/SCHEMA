Label hierarchy and minimal required outputs: 1) Strong labels (full pixel masks) for a stratified subset of cases; 2) Weak labels (scribbles, points, bounding boxes) for a larger pool; 3) Auxiliary labels: object-scale metadata (estimated diameter in mm or percentile), edge maps or signed distance transforms (exported automatically), and per-case annotator confidence (0–3).

Sampling and allocation strategy: Stratify the labeled pool by scale category using dataset percentiles (small: <25th, medium: 25–75th, large: >75th) and by rarity/pathology. Allocate two independent expert annotators per strong-label case. For simulated scarcity regimes (1%, 5%, 10%), ensure proportional representation across scale bins and rare classes to avoid biasing scale learning.

Annotation workflow and tooling: Use an interactive segmentation UI with model-assisted pre-segmentation and quick correction tools (brush, smart-lasso, scribbles). For weak labels use scribbles/centroid clicks with a short-form instruction set. Export masks plus edge maps and SDTs, and record annotator ID, time spent, tool actions, and per-case confidence.

Consensus and adjudication: Require two independent annotations for each strong-label case. Compute quantitative agreement (Dice and 95% surface distance). If Dice ≥ 0.80 and surface distance within acceptable bound, accept consensus (average or union depending on clinical preference). If Dice < 0.80 or clinically significant disagreement, send to a senior adjudicator who produces the final mask and records the rationale. Flag cases that remain ambiguous with an "uncertain" label to exclude from strict supervision or to treat with softer per-pixel weights.

Inter-rater variability monitoring and QC: Continuously log Dice, boundary F1, adjudication rate, and time-per-case. Perform senior-audits on a random 10% of annotations and every case with high model-annotator disagreement. Track annotator drift and retrain annotators with periodic calibration sessions using a small gold-standard set.

Weak supervision and time-saving measures: Collect scribbles/points broadly; use interactive corrections to convert model proposals into high-quality masks quickly. Allow single-annotator weak marks with periodic spot-checks. Use annotated centroids/bboxes to derive approximate scale priors for L_scale_reg.

Active/uncertainty-driven allocation: Use teacher uncertainty maps and scale-consensus diagnostics to prioritize next full annotations: select high-uncertainty pixels/regions, underrepresented scale bins, and high-clinical-impact cases.
