First, to establish defensible semantic-similarity and automated accuracy thresholds (currently ≥0.70 and 60/85%), I recommend a 2–4 week pilot specifically collecting a large labeled corpus of generated factual claims paired with retrieved evidence (targeting 800–1,200 pairs), alongside 1,500–3,000 human-labeled student item responses stratified across grade bands and ELL/low-SES subgroups. Using these data, we will construct ROC curves for semantic similarity metrics and automated scoring outputs, selecting optimal cutpoints by maximizing Youden’s J statistic while constraining false-positive rates to limit human-review workload to ≤10%. This approach ensures thresholds are empirically grounded, balancing precision and practical review capacity.

Second, for validating the hallucination detection system, we will develop a clear annotation protocol with mutually exclusive labels: Supported, Unsupported (hallucinated), Unverifiable, and Harmful. All items will be double-annotated with adjudication by a third expert to achieve an inter-rater reliability κ≥0.80. Evaluation metrics will focus on precision, recall, and F1 score specifically for detecting Unsupported outputs, supplemented by ROC/AUC for continuous detector scores. A retraining cadence of weekly updates during pilot operations and monthly thereafter will maintain detector robustness. To address risk tiers, we will implement a tiered human-review SLA: Tier 1 (harmful content) requires review within 4 hours, Tier 2 (factual errors compromising comprehension) within 24 hours, and Tier 3 (minor stylistic/coherence issues) within 48 hours. All flagged data and review logs will be securely retained for two years to ensure auditability and ethical oversight.

Third, regarding the SDT-based reading motivation composite, we will integrate validated instruments including the Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction scale adapted for reading contexts, select Intrinsic Motivation Inventory subscales (Interest/Enjoyment and Perceived Competence), and a concise Reading Engagement Scale. These subscales will be standardized and aggregated, with internal consistency (α or ω) targeted at ≥0.80. The pre-registered analytic plan will specify mediation testing of condition effects on comprehension gains via motivation composite, and moderation analyses by ELL status and baseline ability, with appropriate multiple-comparison corrections applied.

Fourth, for power and sensitivity to detect effects per grade versus pooled analyses, we will run comprehensive multilevel simulation scripts varying ICCs (0.01, 0.05, 0.10), cluster sizes (15–25), and effect sizes (d = 0.25–0.40). Initial calculations indicate ~25 clusters per arm per grade band achieve 80% power to detect d ≈ 0.30 at ICC=0.05, while pooled analyses enable lower per-grade cluster counts but require stratified secondary analyses.

Fifth, automated scoring robustness will be ensured through rater training protocols requiring ≥8 hours of calibration and an ICC ≥ 0.80 before independent scoring. We will curate a labeled corpus of ≥1,000 short constructed responses per grade band, with oversampling of ELL and low-SES students (≥20–30%) to enhance fairness and generalization. Validation targets include Pearson correlations ≥0.80 and weighted κ ≥0.75. If unmet, incremental expansions of the labeled training corpus by 50% will be pursued.

Sixth, our anchor-equating design will comprise 6–8 mixed cognitive-demand items spanning literal, inferential, and evaluative processes, carefully spread across difficulty parameters (b ≈ −1.5 to +1.5 logits). Power for DIF detection will be optimized by recruiting 200–300 examinees per subgroup, with oversampling by 20% of ELL/FRL status. DIF flags will follow established thresholds (Mantel-Haenszel Δ|≥0.43, ΔR^2≥0.02, item parameter difference >0.10) guiding item revision or removal.

Finally, to harden privacy and data governance, we will implement strong key management with KMS/HSM-backed keys and 90-day rotation schedules, maintain strict least-privilege RBAC policies, and store linkage keys offline with secure purge protocols for right-to-erasure requests completed within 7 calendar days. Data sharing will incorporate differential privacy or synthetic data techniques, consulting closely with the Data Privacy Lead to balance utility and compliance with FERPA/GDPR. All security and triage SOPs will be version-controlled and disseminated prior to pilot start.

I will collaborate closely with Psychometrics, Safety Lead, and Data Systems personnel to deliver exemplar prompt→generation→scoring pipelines, annotation guides, ROC pilot reports, detector evaluation, and pre-registration materials within agreed 1–4 week milestones. This empirically grounded, safety-conscious, and privacy-compliant approach will ensure that the pilot calibration robustly informs threshold setting, detector governance, composite motivation measurement, power justification, automated scoring fairness, anchor equating stability, and data stewardship—all essential to underpin rigorous, scalable deployment and research validity.
