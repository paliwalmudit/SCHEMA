We will implement a hierarchical imputation protocol: (1) use reported N for the relevant subgroup; (2) reconstruct N from tables/flow diagrams; (3) contact authors with up to two attempts, seven days apart; (4) infer from similar subgroups within the paper; (5) if unresolved, impute a conservative N of 20 and flag. For missing variances or reliabilities, we will perform multiple imputation with 50 imputations and apply Rubin’s rules, reporting pooled estimates and sensitivity bounds across alternative imputations. Uncertainty from imputations will be captured in the reported CIs.

4) Publication-bias methods and convergence. We will employ PET-PEESE and contour plots as primary diagnostics, then Vevea-Hedges weight-function models with cluster adaptations; when feasible, Bayesian selection models in Stan with weakly informative priors. Convergence will be assessed via R̂, effective sample size, and trace plots; if convergence fails, we will default to robust cluster bootstrapping and report limitations transparently.

5) Propagating measurement reliability. When possible, we use composite reliability (omega) or test–retest estimates; otherwise we apply attenuation corrections and propagate uncertainty via multiple imputation or bootstrap (e.g., 1000–2000 replicates). We will report both corrected effects and uncertainty intervals reflecting reliability bounds.

6) Dependence in conversions. We will explicitly model within-study correlations when converting dependent effects, conducting sensitivity over rho values (0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.8) and reporting results for each. When designs involve clustering, we will adjust Neff using plausible ICC ranges and perform sensitivity analyses across those ranges.

7) Mixed-methods integration mechanics. We will use a convergent synthesis matrix with themes coded as binary or ordinal moderators; IRR targets of ≥0.80 will guide coding reliability. We will require themes to appear in at least 10% of studies to be included as moderators and present a joint display linking dominant themes to quantitative heterogeneity.

8) IoT technical moderator parsimony. We predefine four primary technical moderators: EHR integration type, interoperability standard, data governance presence, and security posture. All other technical fields remain exploratory, reducing overfitting risk while preserving potential explanatory insights.

9) Equity and language considerations. We will assess feasibility for non-English studies; if included, we will code equity/socioeconomic moderators (SES proxies, digital literacy, urban/rural status) and perform subgroup or meta-regression analyses to detect language or selection biases.

10) Reproducibility and preregistration. Protocols will be preregistered (PROSPERO/OSF), software/packages versioned, and analytic scripts plus de-identified extraction datasets shared in a reproducible repository. We will deliver pilot code templates and diagnostic reports within the planned week-by-week milestones.
